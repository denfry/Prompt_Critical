Everything starts with a piece of iron forged by hand. Plant machines will later be assembled from parts, and the first parts come from a smith: a hearth, an anvil, a hammer and a bucket of water.

## The bloomery

Charge the [bloomery](item:promptcritical:bloomery) with raw iron and charcoal and pump the bellows with the button on its panel. The blast dies down in a few seconds, so keep it up. Above 1100 °C the charcoal reduces the ore, but the iron does not melt: it sticks together into a spongy **bloom**, half of it slag. Do not overdo it: above 1350 °C for more than five minutes the iron picks up carbon and you get [cast iron](item:promptcritical:pig_iron) instead of a bloom.

## Consolidating the bloom

Heat the bloom in the [forge hearth](item:promptcritical:forge_hearth) to white heat, 1200 °C and up, and strike it on the [anvil](item:promptcritical:smithing_anvil). Every series of blows at white heat squeezes some slag out; colder blows leave it in the metal. When no slag is left you have wrought iron.

## Forging

Keep a [smith's hammer](item:promptcritical:smith_hammer) in your inventory. Pick an operation: drawing out lengthens and thins, upsetting shortens and thickens, bending keeps the size. The Strike button is a series of eight blows. Click timing does not matter; your decisions do.

- The glow colour tells the temperature. Forge within the forging range: for steel 45 that is 1200…800 °C. A cold blow cracks the piece, and a crack is scrap.
- Do not upset a long bar: longer than two and a half thicknesses, it buckles.
- Too hot is bad too: above the solidus the steel burns, and that cannot be fixed.

A 20 kg bloom is too much for a hand hammer. **Cut in half** splits a hot piece at the anvil; the other half lands in the second slot. Work pieces of one to three kilograms.

**Forge weld** joins the pieces in both slots: both at white heat, about 1300 °C, with sand in your inventory as flux. Without flux, or a little too cold, the seam keeps a lack of fusion that stays in the passport.

Carry hot pieces with [smith's tongs](item:promptcritical:tongs) in your inventory. Without them anything above 60 °C burns your hands, and red heat burns hard.

## Quenching and tempering

Quench with the button on the anvil panel; there must be water next to it, a source or a cauldron. Thin steel 45 hardens through, a thick bar only at the surface. Water can crack alloy and high-carbon steel. Oil is gentler: a pool of crude oil next to the anvil, or a tank holding at least a bucket of crude, vacuum gas oil, light cycle oil or diesel. It rarely cracks, but it hardens through only thin pieces.

Temper at the edge of the forge hearth, which gets about a third of the fire's heat. Watch the temper colour: straw is about 230 °C, blue about 300 °C. The hotter and longer the temper, the softer and tougher the metal. A second temper hotter than the first softens it further; a colder one changes almost nothing.

## The shaft

A cold piece, round or square and at least four thicknesses long, is handed over by the anvil as a **shaft**, the first part with a passport. The passport holds the material, heat treatment, hardness and defects; measured values show after inspection.

## Wire

The [draw plate](item:promptcritical:draw_plate) pulls a copper ingot, or a cold round rod of wrought iron or plain steel up to 12 mm, through a row of dies. Keep tongs in your inventory and pick a die a size smaller than the wire. The windlass gives 1500 N: copper and thin iron go, a steel rod does not. A honeycomb in the second slot waxes the die for eight passes and cuts the force.

Every pass work-hardens the wire. Do not take more than about a third of the area in one pass, or it breaks. When it breaks from hardening, anneal it in the fire of the forge hearth and carry on.

Both have full-size versions built on a construction site from their drawings, with the same work inside: the [clay bloomery](item:promptcritical:bloomery_real), 1.7 m high with its pair of bellows on the west, is charged from the top and the bloom comes out under the arch at the front. At the [smith's forge hearth](item:promptcritical:forge_hearth_real), with its hood, its 3 m chimney and the great bellows on the east, the work goes into the fire from the front.
