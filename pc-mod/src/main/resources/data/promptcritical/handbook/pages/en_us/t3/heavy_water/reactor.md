## Heavy water in CANDU

A new CANDU is dry, and a dry one is not critical: natural uranium needs a moderator. Pipe heavy water in from below to fill the heat transport circuit (about 3 t), and from any other side to fill the calandria (about 112 t, a hundred buckets).

The moderator level matters. With the calandria half full the upper channels sit in gas and barely fission, so the core behaves as if it were half as tall. At Pickering, dumping the moderator into a tank was a way to shut down. A half-empty heat transport circuit is the same as voided channels: positive reactivity and poor cooling.

Purity matters too. Each atom-percent of protium in the moderator costs about 12 mk, more than the whole reserve that on-power refuelling keeps. Light water gets in through leaks or a wrong pipe. The desk shows purity and lights **D₂O diluted**.

The **Drain** button sends 1 m³ of moderator into a tank of its grade. Pipe that to the finishing column and pipe the reactor-grade product back. Tritium leaves with the drained water, so the specific activity in the calandria stays the same.
