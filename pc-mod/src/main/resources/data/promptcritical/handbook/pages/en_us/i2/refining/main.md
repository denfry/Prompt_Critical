Crude oil is a mixture of thousands of hydrocarbons, each boiling at its own temperature. Atmospheric distillation splits it by boiling range into gasoline, kerosene, diesel and fuel oil.

## The TBP curve

What a crude will yield is shown by its true boiling point curve: the share that boils off up to each temperature. A light West Siberian crude gives 25 % below 180 °C and 53.5 % below 350 °C, and the rest is residue. The cut points are standard: gasoline up to 180 °C, kerosene 180–240 °C, diesel 240–350 °C, fuel oil above.

## The unit

A [crude heater](item:promptcritical:tube_furnace) takes crude and associated gas through Create pipes. The column stands within 6 blocks of it: the [column bottom](item:promptcritical:crude_column), at least three [tray sections](item:promptcritical:column_tray_section) on top, and a [column top](item:promptcritical:column_top). Each section counts as two theoretical trays. Products are drawn off by height: gasoline from the top, kerosene from the upper half of the sections, diesel from the lower half, fuel oil from the bottom.

## The heater

The hotter the crude leaves the heater, the more of it flashes in the feed zone. Whatever stays liquid ends up in the residue. At 360 °C nearly all the light products come off. At 320 °C part of the diesel stays in the fuel oil. Nobody heats crude above 380 °C, because it starts to crack. The heater needs about a megajoule per kilogram of crude, and burning associated gas provides it.

## How sharp the cut is

A column doesn't slice crude cleanly at a temperature. Neighbouring products overlap: kerosene carries a tail of diesel, and diesel carries the front end of kerosene. How cleanly they separate depends on the number of trays and the reflux ratio. The column is solved by the Fenske–Underwood–Gilliland method: minimum trays at total reflux, minimum reflux, and the correlation between them. The Reflux buttons on the panel change the reflux. More reflux gives cleaner products but costs heater duty, because the reflux has to be boiled up again. The panel shows the kerosene/diesel overlap in kelvin.
