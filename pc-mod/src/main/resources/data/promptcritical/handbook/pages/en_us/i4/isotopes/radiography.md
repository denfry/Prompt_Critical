## Sealed source

An irradiated, intact cobalt or iridium capsule is rolled into a second steel shell (an iron ingot) on a crafting table, and that makes a special-form [sealed source](item:promptcritical:sealed_source). The tooltip shows its activity and IAEA category. The category compares activity to the D-value, the amount that could cause severe harm unshielded: 30 GBq for Co-60, 80 GBq for Ir-192. A few hundred TBq irradiator is category 1, a 1–4 TBq radiography source is category 2. A source in your inventory irradiates you, and hard.

## Gamma radiography camera

The [camera](item:promptcritical:radiography_camera) is a lead container with an S-shaped channel and a crank. Load it by sneak-using it with the source in your off hand; unload with the same action and an empty off hand. Inside the container 3.7 TBq of iridium gives about 20 µSv/h at a metre.

To inspect a welded pipe, hold the pipe in your off hand and keep [film](item:promptcritical:radiography_film) in your inventory. Hold use: the crank pushes the source out to the weld and the exposure runs, with the percentage shown above the hotbar. Let go and the source winds back, and the film develops.

The time needed depends on source activity, distance (0.5 m) and wall thickness, since steel attenuates the gamma rays. The film is good between half and twice the required exposure. Underexposed film is clear, overexposed film is black, and neither shows anything.

Good film shows what radiography sees: porosity, lack of fusion, hook cracks, shrinkage cavities. Laminations and eccentricity are ultrasonic findings and don't appear. An inspected pipe gets the RT mark. An uninspected weld may hide a defect, and a defective weld under pressure bursts.

While the source is out it is bare, half a metre from your face. A long exposure doses the radiographer; in real life that is how radiographers most often got overexposed.
