# NPD: the first CANDU

Nuclear Power Demonstration, Rolphton, Ontario, 1962: 92 MW of heat, 22 MW of electricity. Every later CANDU grew out of it. 132 horizontal Zircaloy pressure tubes run through an aluminium calandria 5.2 m across and 4.6 m long. The calandria holds cold heavy water at almost atmospheric pressure, which is the moderator. Hot heavy water under pressure flows through the tubes and carries the heat to the boilers.

NPD has no control rods. Its power is held by the height of the moderator in the calandria: channels above the water are not moderated and hardly fission. The shutdown system is the moderator itself. A dump opens and the heavy water falls into a tank below.

## What you build

- **The reactor** (7 × 7 × 7). The heat transport headers are at the corners of both faces, the moderator dump tank connects at the bottom and its drain at the top. The board is on the controller.
- **Two fuelling machines**, one in front of each face, with their backs to the reactor. One machine is not enough: the fresh fuel goes in at one end and the spent fuel comes out at the other.
- **Heat transport pumps and boilers** within 24 blocks. The pumps run from a Create shaft, and the boilers raise steam for the turbine just like a steam generator.

The fuel is the 19-element NPD bundle of natural uranium, about 14 kg of uranium each. Put bundles in the board's fresh slot: they go into the fuel bay, up to 27.

Heavy water goes in through the ports: into the heat transport circuit through the headers, and into the dump tank from below. Any grade is accepted, but light water dilutes it, and reactivity falls about 20 mk per percent of light water in the moderator.
