## Electrolysis

The [reduction pot](item:promptcritical:reduction_pot) is a steel shell lined with carbon cathode blocks, with carbon anodes hanging from a frame above. Alumina dissolves at 2–5 % in molten cryolite at 950–965 °C, and the current does the rest: 2Al₂O₃ + 3C → 4Al + 3CO₂. Load alumina, prebaked anodes and cryolite by hand or hopper. Aluminium comes out in 100 kg ingots at the bottom.

The direct current comes from an [electrolysis rectifier](item:promptcritical:rectifier_unit) within 6 blocks. Pots within 4 blocks of each other form one series: the same current flows through all of them and their voltages add up. Set the series current on the pot the rectifier feeds. Electrolysis runs on real time, like everything electric. At 150 kA, 93 % current efficiency and 4.3 V a pot makes 46.8 kg of aluminium per hour and draws 645 kW, which is 13.8 kWh of DC per kilogram. Twenty-four such pots take 15.5 MW. A tonne of aluminium also eats 0.40–0.45 t of anodes and 15–25 kg of fluorides.

## Starting a pot

A new pot is cold. Pass current through it to bake the cathode; that takes a couple of hours at full current. Once the pot is at 900 °C, charge 10 bags of cryolite as the starting bath; at 950 °C it goes into normal operation. Cryolite is precipitated in the [ADU precipitator](item:promptcritical:precipitation_tank) from aluminium hydroxide, anhydrous HF and caustic when there is no uranium eluate to work.

## Anode effect

The point feeder keeps alumina at 3 % from the hopper. If alumina runs out and falls below 1.8 %, the anodes stop being wetted by the bath: the anode effect. The pot jumps to about 30 V, current efficiency falls, and CF₄ and C₂F₆ go up the stack. These are counted in the emission ledger at 6630 and 11 100 times the warming of CO₂. Feed alumina and the effect dies.

## Freezing

Without current the bath cools. A minute without power is harmless. After that the pot is freezing, and within three to six minutes it reaches the liquidus at 940 °C and freezes solid. A frozen pot does not conduct and the series bypasses it. The only way back is a reline: 4 cathode blocks and 8 refractory bricks in the load, then the Reline button, then a fresh bake. This is why aluminium smelters want two independent supplies and automatic transfer.

The cathode also wears with the charge it carries. When it is worn through, molten metal runs out of the pot: cathode failure, fire around the shell, and another reline.
