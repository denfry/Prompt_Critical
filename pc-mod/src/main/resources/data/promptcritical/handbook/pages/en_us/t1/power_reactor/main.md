On 27 June 1954 the AM-1 reactor in Obninsk sent power into the grid: 30 MW of heat, 5 MW of electricity, the world's first nuclear power plant. It is built like a graphite pile, but its channels hold enriched fuel and the heat is carried away by water at 10 MPa.

## The reactor

The [channel reactor](item:promptcritical:power_reactor) is a 3×6×3 graphite stack. At the bottom is the core, ⌀1.5 m and 1.7 m high, with 128 channels on a 120 mm pitch and a graphite reflector; on top are the control rod drives. Each channel takes an AM-1 assembly: four tubular fuel elements around a central water supply tube. Water goes down the central tube and up through the elements. Assemblies come from the fuel shop, AM-1 type on the press and the jig. Put a fresh assembly in the panel slot or feed it with a hopper, and on a shut-down reactor it goes into the first empty channel from the centre. The Unload key takes out the most burnt channel.

The fuel is 5 %, about 4.4 kg of uranium per channel and 560 kg in the core, enough for about 100 days at full power. Central channels burn faster than the edge ones because the flux falls off towards the edge as a Bessel function. A spent assembly carries its burnup (MWd/kg), the Pu-239 it bred and its fission products.

## Feedback

Hot fuel absorbs more neutrons in the U-238 resonances (Doppler effect), and reactivity falls. Hot graphite gives a negative effect too. The water in AM-1 channels is a real part of the moderator: in 1954 the reactor went critical with 60 channels filled with water, while without water it would have needed 101. So hot, light and above all boiling water lowers reactivity.

Xenon-135 builds up from the decay of iodine-135 and absorbs neutrons more greedily than anything else. At full power it takes about 3 % of reactivity. After shutdown the iodine keeps decaying into xenon and nothing burns it out: xenon grows and peaks 8–12 hours later. This is the iodine pit, and you cannot restart until the xenon decays. Poisons and burnup run on game time, kinetics and heat on real time.
