## Making it

**GS plant (3×6×3).** Water and hydrogen sulphide swap hydrogen isotopes: H₂O + HDS ⇄ HDO + H₂S. In the cold tower (30 °C) the water pulls deuterium out of the gas with a factor of 2.32. In the hot tower (130 °C) it gives some back, but only with 1.80. The gas circulates between the two, and enriched water builds up between them.

The waste cannot get leaner than the hot tower's own equilibrium, so at most 1 − 1.80/2.32 ≈ 22 % of the deuterium in the feed can be extracted. Real plants got 18–20 %. The rest goes back to the lake.

That is why the plant needs a lake: it draws a tonne of water a second. Build it on the shore so that at least 32 water source blocks lie within four blocks of its base, one to three layers down. Fewer blocks mean proportionally less feed. A hotter hot tower extracts more deuterium, up to the 140 °C limit. The product, 15 % D, comes out into pipes.

**Finishing column (1×3×1).** Vacuum distillation: D₂O is slightly less volatile than H₂O, by a factor of about 1.05 at 60 °C. Hence the 400 theoretical plates and the vacuum. Pipe in 15 % water or downgraded heavy water and reactor grade comes out. Natural water is too lean for it; send that through the GS plant first.
