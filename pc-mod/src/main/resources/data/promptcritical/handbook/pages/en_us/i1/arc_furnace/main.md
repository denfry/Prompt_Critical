An [electric arc furnace](item:promptcritical:electric_arc_furnace) melts steel scrap with electricity: three graphite electrodes strike an arc onto the charge, and the arc heats it to 1620 °C. Unlike the converter, it needs no hot metal and no oxygen: it runs on offcuts, rejects and scrap. It does need the grid.

## Operation

Bring a 6 kV cable to the shell and charge [steel scrap](item:promptcritical:steel_scrap) by hand, up to 6 t. Right-click lowers the electrodes and strikes the arc. Put a [ladle](item:promptcritical:ladle) in front of the spout: once everything has melted and reached 1620 °C, the furnace taps by itself. To tap earlier, once all is liquid, use Shift and right-click.

## How much energy

Heating a tonne of steel to the solidus, melting it and superheating it takes 386 kWh. An open arc without water cooling gives about 65 % of its power to the charge, so in practice it takes about 600 kWh per tonne. The furnace draws about 3.2 MW, and a 6 t heat takes about an hour. Electrodes wear at about 5 kg per tonne.

## The grid

The arc is a resistance behind the short-network reactance. The electrode regulator holds a power factor of about 0.8, so along with 3.2 MW the furnace draws about 2.4 Mvar. Until the scrap settles, the arc breaks and jumps, and the reactive power swings by half. The bus voltage then swings too, and the neighbours' lamps flicker. On a flat bath the arc calms down. Put the furnace on a strong bus, through its own transformer, away from the village. A 500 kVA generator cannot even strike the arc.
