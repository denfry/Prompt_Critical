## The primary loop

Water at 10 MPa enters the channels at 190 °C and leaves at 280 °C; 30 MW takes about 70 kg/s. It is driven by [primary circulation pumps](item:promptcritical:primary_pump) on Create shafts, each good for half the flow, within 12 blocks of the reactor. A stopped pump still coasts for about fifteen seconds on its flywheel; with no pumps only natural circulation is left, about 5 %.

The [steam generator](item:promptcritical:steam_generator) stands against a secondary boiler, either a fire-tube boiler or a water-tube drum. The primary heat goes into the boiler water, and the boiler feeds steam to the same turbines as a coal plant. With no boiler, or with the boiler on its relief valve, the heat has nowhere to go: the loop heats up and the reactor shuts itself down by feedback or by the outlet temperature trip.

## Dryout

If the water in a channel nearly all boils away (exit quality above 0.25), the fuel element wall dries out and can no longer shed its heat. After five seconds of dryout the cladding fails. The channel is broken: water and steam reach the graphite and add reactivity (water in the stack moderates), and fission products escape into the hall. The Channel lamp lights and the dosimeter starts crackling with I-131.

On a loss of flow with protections in service, the flow trip shuts the reactor down before the pumps stop. Natural circulation then carries away the decay heat: about 6 % at once and about 1.5 % after an hour. With protections bypassed, channels burn.

The reactor itself is a radiation source: neutrons and gamma rays come from the core in proportion to fission power, and after shutdown from the fission products. Build the biological shield from blocks: concrete, water, lead, earth.
