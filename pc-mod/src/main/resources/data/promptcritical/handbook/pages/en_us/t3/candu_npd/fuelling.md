## On-power refuelling

Natural uranium has little reactivity to spare, so NPD is refuelled at power, a few bundles at a time, without shutting down.

Each machine needs power from a board or a socket (60 kW while working) and heavy water in its head tank through the west port. The machines take fresh bundles from the bay into their magazines, eight at most. The "±" button sets how many go in per visit: 2, 4 or 8.

"Fuel" sends both machines to the most burnt channel. They travel to it, lock their snouts onto its end fittings, fill their heads with heavy water and raise them to coolant pressure. Then they pull the closure plugs and the ram pushes the fresh bundles in one by one. Neighbouring channels flow opposite ways, so the fuel always goes in with the flow, sometimes from the north face and sometimes from the south. Spent bundles come out of the far end into the other magazine. After that the plugs go back, the pressure comes off and the machines return to the bay. A visit takes about ten minutes with four bundles and about a quarter of an hour with eight. Pressing "Fuel" again during a visit aborts it: the machines close the channel and come back.

The spent bundles leave the board's output slot as one lot when the machines are home and the slot is empty.

## What can go wrong

- **No power**: the machines stop where they are, even locked onto a channel.
- **No heavy water at the heads**: the machines wait at the pressurising step. The fuelling machine readout turns gold.
- **Seal leak**: while the heads are at pressure, a little heavy water from the circuit leaks past the snout seals. It carries tritium.
- **Uncooled spent fuel**: spent bundles in the magazine still give off heat. Without heavy water flowing through the head they overheat in tens of minutes and their cladding fails. Take the spent fuel out before you cut the head water.
