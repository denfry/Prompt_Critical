## The cascade

One machine enriches little: its separation factor is 1.1–1.5. So machines are joined into a cascade where each stage sends its product one stage up and its tails one stage down. The [cascade control](item:promptcritical:centrifuge_cascade) counts the centrifuge sections within 8 blocks and builds an ideal cascade from them for the set product and tails assays. The feed stage is the widest, and the cascade narrows towards both ends. Making 4.5 % from natural uranium takes about twenty stages with at least one machine each; with fewer machines the Few mach. lamp comes on.

Hang the feed as a UF₆ cylinder in the first panel slot. An empty 30B or 5A goes under the product and an empty 48Y under the tails; hoppers work too. The product first condenses in a desublimer and is drained only into a cylinder that will take it: a 30B up to 5 %, a 5A any enrichment but only 25 kg. Depleted UF₆ from the tails is a product of its own, later made into alloys and armour.

A Create shaft at the back drives the machines through a frequency converter. Every SWU takes about 50 kWh, and machines without power stand still.

## Criticality safety

Dry UF₆ will not run away by itself: it needs a moderator. But moisture leaking into the desublimer turns it into a UO₂F₂ solution, and then the limit is the minimum critical mass of U-235: about 2 kg at 5 %, about 1 kg at 20 %, 0.8 kg for weapons-grade uranium. Below 0.95 % uranium in water is never critical.

The criticality interlock stops the product draw when the U-235 in the desublimer reaches 45 % of the minimum critical mass, the safe mass. A panel key can switch it off, and then a desublimer with no cylinder keeps collecting product until it goes critical. A self-sustaining chain reaction follows: a blue flash, no explosion, but a neutron and gamma dose to everyone around. The dose falls with the square of the distance, and a few gray next to the panel kill. That is how it went at Tokaimura in 1999.
