## How hydrogen burns

The zirconium of a hot core and a melt on concrete both put hydrogen into the containment. What it does there depends on what it is mixed with. The accident tooltip on the reactor panel shows the hydrogen and steam fractions and how the mixture would burn if lit now.

**Will it burn at all.** Below the lower limit, 4 % plus 0.11 of the steam fraction, it will not. Above 55 % steam it will not, whatever the hydrogen: a hot containment full of steam is inert until the sprays or the walls condense the steam. Below 5 % oxygen it will not either. That is why a boiling water reactor's containment is filled with nitrogen.

**How it burns.** Between the limit and 8 % a flame only climbs upwards and burns part of the gas. From 8 % it burns all of it, and the pressure rises to the AICC value: all the heat into the gas, with half a mole less gas for every mole of hydrogen burnt. In dry air that is 3.5 times the starting pressure at 8 %, 4.2 times at 10 %, 5.8 times at 15 %. From 10 % the flame accelerates. From 13 % a large room lets it turn into a detonation, once the room is more than seven detonation cells across. A cell is 1 m wide at 13 %, 30 cm at 15 % and 1 cm at the stoichiometric 30 %. A detonation hits the walls with about twice the AICC pressure.

**When it lights.** Once the whole volume reaches 8 %, something always sparks. A hot surface lights any flammable mixture straight away: corium out of the vessel, or the droplets of a high-pressure melt ejection. In a containment full of steam, hydrogen builds up silently past 8 %, and it all goes at once when the steam condenses or the corium arrives.

**What to do.** Recombiners eat hydrogen without a flame, about 1.5 kg an hour each at 4 %. A small containment fills up faster: the zirconium of the VVER core alone makes 30 kg of hydrogen, 13 % of a 2500 m³ building.
