# Vacuum distillation

Mazut from the bottom of the crude column still holds half its mass in distillable gas oil, but it would have to be heated above 550 °C to boil off, and above 410 °C it cracks and cokes the furnace tubes. The way out is vacuum: under 5 kPa a fraction boils some 190 K colder. With Trouton's rule (ΔS ≈ 87 J/(mol·K)) a cut that boils at T_b at atmospheric pressure boils under p at T_b/(1 − ln(p/p₀)/10.5): at 400 °C and 5 kPa everything up to about 590 °C goes over.

The [vacuum column](item:promptcritical:vacuum_column) has its own furnace, fired with sales gas, and gives two products: vacuum gas oil (350–550 °C) from the top, the feed of the catalytic cracker, and tar (vacuum residue, above 550 °C) from the bottom. Tar carries all the resins and asphaltenes of the mazut: its Conradson carbon is about twice that of mazut, around 20 %, and the delayed coker now takes tar first and makes more coke from it.

Vacuum comes from [steam ejectors](item:promptcritical:steam_ejector) touching the column: one stage holds 8 kPa in the flash zone, two hold 5 kPa, three hold 3 kPa. Their motive steam (about 2 % of the mazut by mass, together with the stripping steam) is raised in the furnace convection coil from boiler feedwater piped to the column. Without ejectors or water the column runs at atmospheric pressure and gives a fraction of the gas oil.
