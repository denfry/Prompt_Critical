The plant has no computers yet, so everything that matters is written by hand: instrument readings on the rounds, the shift handover, permission to work on equipment that has been switched off. Paper survives any accident, and afterwards it shows who saw what and who allowed what.

## Shift log

The [shift log](item:promptcritical:shift_log) is a bound book. The operator on duty walks the plant and right-clicks a machine with the log to write a line: day and time, the machine, and its panel readings — dials, scales, levels, lit lamps. The log is kept in order: no line can be torn out or corrected, and each new one goes under the last.

A shift is handed over with a signature. Shift + use with the log in hand writes "Shift handed over" with your name and the time, and the next watch starts on a clean line. If nothing was written this shift, there is nothing to hand over.

A full log (200 entries) goes to the archive, and you start a new one. Put the log on the [document lectern](item:promptcritical:document_lectern) by the control desk and anyone who walks up can read it.

## Work permit

Work on switched-off equipment in an electrical installation starts with a [work permit](item:promptcritical:work_permit). It is written on the apparatus that makes the break, a breaker or a disconnector. The order follows the electrical safety rules:

1. The apparatus is switched off. Right-click it with a blank form to write the permit: the drive is locked and the poster "Do not switch on! People at work" goes up. A closed apparatus will not take a permit, so switch it off first. Never open a disconnector under load; take the current off with the breaker.
2. Absence of voltage is proved. There should be none beyond the break.
3. Earthing is applied. On a disconnector, that is the earthing switch (Shift + use on the disconnector).
4. Admission. Right-click the apparatus with the permit: if every measure is in place, the brigade is admitted. If not, the permit tells you what is missing.

While the permit is open, nothing switches the apparatus on, neither the key nor the automatics. The breaker drive has its control power withdrawn, so auto-reclose cannot close it either. The earthing cannot be removed under an open permit.

When the work is done, Shift + use with the permit on the apparatus closes it. Then remove the earthing and switch on. A closed permit can go on the lectern.

If a permit is lost, the apparatus stays locked. It is closed on the second copy: Shift + use with a blank form on the locked apparatus.

## Why it matters

A shock from a busbar is real here: the current follows from the touch voltage and the body's resistance. A dead busbar is safe only as long as nobody energises it. The permit with its locked drive, and the earthing, are what keep it dead while you work on it.
