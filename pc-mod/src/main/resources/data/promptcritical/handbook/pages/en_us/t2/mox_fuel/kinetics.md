## A MOX core is quicker on its feet

Of every hundred neutrons from a U-235 fission, 0.65 come late, seconds to a minute after the fission. Pu-239 gives only 0.21. Those few late neutrons are what lets operators and rods keep up with a reactor at all. A MOX core has noticeably fewer of them: β_eff drops from about 0.0065 to 0.0035–0.0045.

The same reactivity is worth more dollars in MOX. A rod pulled a bit too far, a cold-water slug or a jump in recirculation flow gives a **shorter period** and a bigger prompt jump than in a uranium core. The period trip comes sooner.

Control also gets weaker. MOX absorbs thermal neutrons much more strongly, so any absorber in the core competes with the fuel and loses:

- dissolved **boron** in the VVER is worth roughly half as much per ppm;
- **rods** lose less, about a fifth to a third of their worth, because a lumped rod catches whatever reaches it;
- the **void and moderator** effects of the water are smaller in magnitude, because the spectrum is harder.

That is why real plants load MOX as a third of the core, not all of it: there is enough uranium around it to keep the scram margin. In a full MOX core, check the shutdown margin before startup with every rod in and the strongest one stuck out.

The AM-1 water feedback comes from the 1954 critical experiment and does not change with MOX: nobody ever loaded plutonium there.
