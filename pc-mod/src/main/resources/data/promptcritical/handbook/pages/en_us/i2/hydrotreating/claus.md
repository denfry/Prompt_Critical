## Sulphur recovery

The [Claus unit](item:promptcritical:claus_unit) turns hydrogen sulphide into elemental sulphur. In the reaction furnace a third of the H₂S burns to SO₂ (H₂S + 1.5 O₂ → SO₂ + H₂O), and two thirds of the sulphur condense straight after the flame. The rest goes through three alumina converters, where 2 H₂S + SO₂ ⇌ 3 S + 2 H₂O runs on toward equilibrium.

The reaction gives off heat, so a colder bed pushes the equilibrium further: half the remaining sulphur converts at 300 °C, 60 % at 240 °C, 65 % at 210 °C. There is a floor, though. Below the sulphur dew point plus 10 K, liquid sulphur fills the catalyst pores and the amber lamp lights. The panel sets the first bed; the second and third run 60 and 75 K colder. At 300/240/225 °C the unit recovers about 97 % of the sulphur.

The air has to burn exactly one third of the H₂S. Every 10 % error in the air ratio costs 3 % recovery and sends more SO₂ up the stack. Trim it with the Air buttons until the ratio lamp goes out.

Sulphur comes out in 10 kg lumps; take them from below with a funnel or hopper. The [contact acid plant](item:promptcritical:acid_plant) burns them in place of pyrite: one lump carries 10 kg of sulphur, about 30 kg of sulphuric acid.
