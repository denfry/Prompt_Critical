A flame safety lamp shows methane but gives poor light. Underground, miners work by electric light: a headpiece on the helmet and an alkaline battery on the belt. After the shift the lamp goes back to the lamp room, where it charges overnight.

## The lamp

The [cap lamp](item:promptcritical:cap_lamp) goes on your head and stays there for the whole shift. The battery is nickel-cadmium: three 10 Ah cells at 3.6 V. The 3.75 V incandescent bulb gives about 30 lm and burns for at least 10 hours, as the Soviet SGG lamps were rated to.

The battery runs on game time. One shift lasts 10 hours, about 8 minutes. For most of the shift the voltage holds on a flat plateau, and near the end the battery drops off it. An incandescent bulb's light goes with the voltage to the power 3.4: at 90 % voltage you keep 70 % of the light, at 80 % less than half. So the lamp does not go out at once, it dims. A lamp left uncharged dies early in the next shift.

The bar under the icon shows the charge. The tooltip shows how much capacity is left and how many cycles the battery has done. Over 2000 cycles the capacity falls to 80 %. A lamp lying idle loses about 0.3 % of its charge a day, twice that somewhere warm.

## The lamp room

The [charging rack](item:promptcritical:lamp_rack) runs off the 0.4 kV grid: bring a cable to any face except the front. It has twelve places, each with its own transformer and rectifier. The battery first charges at a constant 2.5 A. Once the voltage reaches 1.55 V per cell, the current tapers off by itself. An alkaline battery needs about a third more charge than it gave out, because the extra goes into splitting water. An empty battery takes 6–7 hours, and all twelve places draw about 140 W from the grid.

If the grid sags below 340 V, charging slows down, and below 200 V it stops altogether. Put a lamp with broken glass on the rack, and the lamp room attendant replaces the glass and the bulb.

## Why the headpiece is flameproof

A mine lamp is made flameproof: the bulb sits under thick glass and the battery is sealed. A long fall, an explosion or a roof fall breaks the glass. The glowing filament of the burst bulb meets the mine air for a split second, and with 5–15 % methane at that spot the mixture explodes. Then the filament burns out and the lamp goes dark.
