## Noise

A machine's sound power grows by 10 dB for every tenfold of shaft power: a 1 MW machine gives 100 dB(A). At the listener the level falls by 6 dB for every doubling of distance, and walls in between take off more: glass 12 dB a block, wood 15, metal 18, stone 22, concrete 25. Sound gets around any wall through gaps and joints, so walls never take off more than 45 dB in total.

Several machines add up by energy, not by decibels: two equal machines are 3 dB louder than one, ten are 10 dB louder. A bearing in stage 3 or 4 adds its own grind on top.

The analyser also shows the noise where you stand.

## Your ears

Hearing damage depends on level and time. The criterion is 85 dB(A) for 8 hours, and every 3 dB more halves the time: 15 minutes at 100 dB(A) is a full day's dose. Below 80 dB(A) nothing builds up. The dose drains away with a half-life of one game day.

Above 85 dB(A) a line over the hotbar warns you. Once the day's dose is used up, your ears ring and you tire and work more slowly. Above 120 dB(A) the noise deafens and staggers you at once.

[Ear muffs](item:promptcritical:hearing_protection) in the head slot cut the level at the ear by 25 dB. They protect your hearing; they do not make the game quieter.
