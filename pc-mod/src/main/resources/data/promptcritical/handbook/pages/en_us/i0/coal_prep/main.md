Run-of-mine coal is not only coal. It holds lumps of roof and floor rock, coal intergrown with rock, and pyrite. Rock does not burn and buries the grate; sulphur spoils metal. Coal preparation separates coal from its impurities.

## What the separation rests on

Density decides everything.

- Coal's organic matter: 1.25–1.40 g/cm³.
- Coal intergrown with rock: in between.
- Rock: above 1.8 g/cm³.
- Pyrite: about 5 g/cm³.

In water, light pieces settle more slowly than heavy ones. That is what a jig works on.

## The coal jig

A [coal jig](item:promptcritical:coal_jig) is a box with a screen through which water is pulsed from below. Each pulse lifts the bed of coal. As it settles, heavy pieces sink faster than light ones. After hundreds of pulses the bed stratifies: rock at the bottom, intergrowths above it, clean coal on top. The layers are drawn off separately.

- Drive: a Create shaft, on the left. At 64 rpm the jig passes 10 kg/s, 36 t/h, and takes about 15 kW. Throughput is proportional to speed.
- Water: 0.5 l per kg of coal, by bucket or pipe. The tank holds 2000 l. Without water the jig stands still.
- Feed: [run-of-mine coal](item:promptcritical:raw_coal), by right-click or hopper. Coal is taken in batches of one assay: the next batch goes in once the current one is washed.
- Empty hand: collect the products and see the feed, water and cut densities.

## Three products

- [Washed coal](item:promptcritical:washed_coal), the clean coal. Ash 6–10 %. Better fuel, and feedstock for coke in the next epoch.
- [Middlings](item:promptcritical:coal_middlings), the intergrowths, ash 25–40 %. They are burnt in boilers.
- [Refuse](item:promptcritical:coal_refuse), to the tip or as backfill in the workings.

## Adjustment

A Create wrench on the jig changes the **cut density** for clean coal: 1.40, 1.50, 1.60 or 1.70 g/cm³. The refuse cut is 1.8.

- A lower cut gives cleaner coal, but less of it: some coal goes to middlings.
- A higher cut gives more clean coal, but intergrowths get in and ash rises.

A jig is not perfect. Pieces near the cut density split at random; some coal is always lost to refuse and some rock always reaches the clean coal. The closer the cut is to the bulk of the coal, the bigger this misplacement.

Sulphur behaves in two ways. Pyritic sulphur, as separate grains of pyrite, leaves with the heavy product. Organic sulphur is bound into the coal itself and stays in the clean coal.
