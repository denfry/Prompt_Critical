# Confined spaces

A tank, a column, a sump: a space a man can get into, with one way in and out, not meant to be stayed in. Set a [manway](item:promptcritical:manway) into its wall. The manway fills the air behind it; if there are no more than 2048 blocks of it, that is a confined space, and the manway keeps its atmosphere — oxygen, nitrogen, carbon dioxide, methane, hydrogen sulphide and carbon monoxide, well mixed.

Gas reaches it through the manway nozzle at 50 L/s: nitrogen or argon from a purge, sales or associated gas, converter gas. Every litre in pushes a litre of what was there out. A 20 m³ vessel purged with nitrogen at 50 L/s is down to 10 % oxygen in five minutes; a leaking valve at 1.7 L/s gets it to 16 % in under an hour. An open hatch breathes 2 L/s; a [portable ventilator](item:promptcritical:portable_ventilator) next to an open hatch blows in 0.5 m³/s. Mixing is never perfect, so count three times the ideal: from 5 % oxygen back to 19.5 % takes about seven volumes of air. The panel shows the gases and how long until the air is fit to enter.

Inside, you breathe what is there. Below 19.5 % oxygen you slow down, below 12 % you go blind and start dying, below 8 % you pass out in ten seconds. Hydrogen sulphide above 100 ppm kills your sense of smell, above 1000 ppm it drops you at once. Carbon monoxide builds up carboxyhaemoglobin with a ten-minute time constant here: 100 ppm settles at 10 %, 1000 ppm at 53 % — unconscious.

A gas mask does not help against a lack of oxygen, and a catalytic flammables sensor in nitrogen reads zero. Only the oxygen cell of the [gas detector](item:promptcritical:portable_gas_detector) tells you the truth.
