## BOR-60: the loop-type fast reactor

The experimental reactor at Dimitrovgrad: 60 MW thermal, with sodium entering the core at 310 °C and leaving at 530 °C. Unlike the pool-type BN, the heat leaves through two loops. Each loop has a main sodium pump, a sodium-sodium intermediate heat exchanger (IHX) and a steam generator. An argon cover gas cushion lies over the primary sodium.

**What the vessel needs nearby:**

- two **main pumps** on Create shafts, one per loop. Primary flow is the mean of the two: if one stops, the flow halves;
- two **IHX**. Each has an electric secondary pump taking 150 kW from the site board. Without power that loop's secondary sodium stands still and its heat stays in the core;
- **steam generators** at boiler drums, as for the BN.

The fuel is one MOX assembly with 15–35 % plutonium, put in by hand or with a hopper.

**Start-up from the board:**

1. Press "Cock" once the scram cause has cleared and the rods are in.
2. Withdraw the control rods with ▲ slowly. Plutonium has almost half the delayed neutron fraction of uranium, so a fast reactor speeds up sharply. Step and stop the rods (■).
3. Watch the core outlet temperature: the scram fires at 570 °C.

The **argon cushion** sits near 150 kPa. The gas system makes up argon below 120 kPa and vents it above 180 kPa. In a cooldown the sodium shrinks and the cushion falls. In a sodium leak the gas takes the freed volume, and the pressure falls too.

The **"Na leak"** lamp lights for a sodium fire and for a sodium-water reaction in a steam generator. The **AZ** key is under a guard: hold it for 1.5 s.
