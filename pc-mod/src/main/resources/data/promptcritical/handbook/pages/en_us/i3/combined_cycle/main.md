A gas turbine throws away more than half of its fuel through the exhaust. The first-generation unit in this game exhausts at 470 °C at full load, a heavy-duty machine of the 1980s at about 540 °C. The [heat recovery steam generator](item:promptcritical:hrsg) catches that heat and turns it into steam, and a steam turbine turns the steam into roughly another half of the gas turbine's output. That is a combined cycle.

## Assembly

The boiler takes 3×6×3: a gas duct below, the drum on top. The duct must touch the turbine end of the [gas turbine](item:promptcritical:gas_turbine), the last of its three sections. The steam goes into a boiler that the heat recovery boiler touches: a fire-tube boiler, or the drum, wall or panel of a water-tube boiler. That boiler sets the pressure and feeds the steam engines and turbines already connected to it, as it does for the electrode boiler and the reactor's steam generator. Shift + use gives a summary; the panel shows the rest.

## Pinch point

Gas cools along the duct through three sections: superheater, evaporator, economizer. The tight spot is the cold end of the evaporator, where the water boils at a fixed temperature. The gas leaving it must stay at least 10 K hotter than the boiling water, otherwise the heat would need an infinitely large surface. This pinch sets the steam flow. The economizer then heats the 104 °C feedwater to 5 K below boiling and cools the gas on its way to the stack. The higher the drum pressure, the hotter the water boils and the more heat goes up the stack: at 4 MPa a 540 °C exhaust leaves at about 190 °C.

At no load a gas turbine burns little and its exhaust drops to about 175 °C. That is colder than water boiling at 15 bar, so there is no steam at all until the turbine takes load. The Gas cold lamp says so.

## What it is worth

The first-generation turbine alone converts 28 % of its fuel into shaft work. With a heat recovery boiler and a condensing steam turbine the plant reaches about 43 %. A 1980s heavy-duty turbine (pressure ratio 12, 1100 °C before the turbine) gives 33 % alone and about 50 % in combined cycle. Plants of the 1990s pushed past 55 % with two and three drum pressures and reheat; this boiler has one pressure and no supplementary firing.
