A fire-tube boiler is a horizontal steel shell full of water. Inside it, below the water line, runs a wide **furnace tube** with the grate at its front end. Hot gases pass through the furnace tube, then through a bank of narrow **smoke tubes**, and leave through the flue and the chimney over the back of the boiler. Water surrounds the tubes and takes up the heat.

## Water and steam

Above the water is the **steam space**. The steam in the boiler is saturated: pressure and temperature are tied together. At 1.2 MPa water boils at about 190 °C. Raising pressure means heating the whole mass of water, 8–10 t of it. So a boiler raises steam slowly and loses it just as slowly: it is a heat store.

The water level shows in the **gauge glass** on the boiler. The red mark on the glass is the lowest permitted level, the height of the furnace tube crown. Water must not drop to it. The crown of the furnace tube is the hottest spot in the boiler. While it is under water, boiling keeps the metal cool. Once it is uncovered, the steel overheats within minutes.

## Feed

Water enters the boiler only through the 4 m³ **feed tank**. Fill the tank with a bucket or a Create pipe into any block of the boiler. The feed regulator moves water from the tank into the boiler by itself: it holds the low level while lighting up and the normal level once pressure passes half of working. The regulator cannot make water out of nothing. An empty tank means nothing to feed the boiler with.

## Furnace and draught

The rated coal feed is 0.3 kg/s, about 7 MW in fuel. At an efficiency near 0.7 that is 5 MW into steam, roughly 6.7 t of steam an hour. One item of coal, 100 kg, burns at rated duty in 333 s.

The chimney pulls air into the furnace. Draught is the difference in weight between a column of hot flue gas and a column of outside air of the same height. A 20 m chimney with gas at 250 °C gives about 110 Pa. A cold chimney draws poorly, which is why the bed is built up gradually. Too little air and the coal burns to carbon monoxide: black smoke, heat going up the chimney unburnt. Too much air and the excess carries heat away, but the smoke stays light.

## Fuel

The boiler burns [run-of-mine coal](item:promptcritical:raw_coal), [washed coal](item:promptcritical:washed_coal), [middlings](item:promptcritical:coal_middlings), ordinary coal and charcoal, and logs. Heat and ash depend on the coal's assay: ashy coal gives less heat and more slag. Ash collects as [boiler slag](item:promptcritical:boiler_slag), 10 kg per item; take it out with an empty hand or a hopper.

## Pressure rises on its own

The boiler does not know how much steam you need. If the engine takes less than the furnace makes, pressure climbs to the setting of the [safety valve](promptcritical:i0/safety_valves). Pressure is controlled with the coal feed, not with the valve.

The gauge shows pressure, the glass shows level. Watch both.
