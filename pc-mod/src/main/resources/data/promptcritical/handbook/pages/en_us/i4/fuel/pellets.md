Enriched uranium leaves the cascade as hexafluoride but goes into a reactor as ceramic: uranium dioxide pellets in zirconium tubes. Every gram of U-235 is on the books along the way. Each station's panel keeps the ledger: how much U-235 came in, how much went on, how much was written off as losses and how much is still in the vessels.

## UO₂ powder

The [conversion line](item:promptcritical:uo2_conversion) follows the wet ammonium diuranate (ADU) route. The UF₆ cylinder is vaporised in an autoclave, the gas is hydrolysed in water to a UO₂F₂ solution, ammonia precipitates ADU, which is filtered and reduced by hydrogen at 600–700 °C to UO₂. ADU powder is fine and fluffy and sinters without additives; the first fuel plants worked this way. Ammonia comes from the nitrogen plant, hydrogen from chlorine electrolysis, and the water is boiler feedwater. The powder leaves in 1 kg uranium bags, each marked with its enrichment, and lots are never mixed.

Uranyl fluoride solution is the most dangerous form of uranium because its water moderates neutrons. So the line keeps the U-235 in its vessels below the safe mass and throttles its own feed. Uranium above 20 % is refused.

## Pellets

The [pellet press](item:promptcritical:pellet_press) on a Create shaft presses the powder into green pellets of about 52 % of theoretical density at 300 MPa. Above 450 MPa the pellets laminate and below 150 MPa they crumble; rejects are crushed back into the hopper. Choose the pellet type on the press: VVER-440, VVER-1000 (with a central hole) or PWR 17×17. The press stands against the furnace.

The [sintering furnace](item:promptcritical:sintering_furnace) sinters the pellets in dry hydrogen. At 1700 °C for four hours they reach 95–96 % of theoretical density (10.4–10.7 g/cm³) with 10 µm grains. Colder or shorter and the pellets stay porous: they densify in the reactor, the gap opens and the fuel runs hot. Without hydrogen the UO₂ picks up oxygen, and an O/U above 2.02 is a reject. Good pellets are ground to size, and sludge and rejects go back to the press in 1 kg uranium bags. Pellets are laid on trays, one tray per rod.
