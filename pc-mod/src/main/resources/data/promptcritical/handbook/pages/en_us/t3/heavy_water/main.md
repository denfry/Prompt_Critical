## Heavy water

In ordinary water one hydrogen atom in 6,400 is deuterium. In heavy water nearly all of them are: reactor grade means 99.75 % D. A litre weighs 1.105 kg against 0.998, and it boils at 101.4 °C.

What a reactor cares about is absorption. Protium catches a thermal neutron 640 times more readily than deuterium, so every quarter-percent of light water left in the moderator eats neutrons that natural uranium cannot spare.

In pipes and tanks the water travels by grade, one fluid per grade, the way a station keeps a separate tank for each:

- **deuterium-enriched water**, 15 % D: what the GS plant makes;
- **downgraded heavy water**, 90 % D: moderator drained from a reactor or collected from leaks;
- **heavy water**, 99.75 % D: reactor grade. Only this one comes in a bucket and can be poured out.

Machines track the real deuterium fraction inside and hand out the highest grade that does not exceed it. Rounding up never creates deuterium.
