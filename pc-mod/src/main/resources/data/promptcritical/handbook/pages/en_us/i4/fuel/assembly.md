## Zirconium

A fuel cladding must hardly absorb neutrons, hold water at 350 °C and not creep. Only zirconium does that, and only once its hafnium is removed: hafnium, its chemical twin, absorbs neutrons 600 times more strongly. Zircon is a heavy mineral in river sand, so it is washed out of sand with Create. Zircon concentrate is reduced by magnesium with coke in a superheated mixer to reactor-grade sponge, and the hafnium separated along the way comes out as an ingot of its own. The sponge is alloyed with tin into a Zircaloy-4 type alloy; the Soviet E110 is zirconium with 1 % niobium. Cladding tubes are rolled in a press, and spacer grids are made at a crafting table.

## The fuel rod

The [rod loading station](item:promptcritical:rod_loading_station) pours a tray of pellets into a cladding tube, sets the spring and the end plug, evacuates the rod, fills it with helium and welds it shut: 2.0 MPa of helium for VVER-1000, 2.4 for PWR. Helium carries heat across the gap from pellet to cladding. Without helium the rods get argon: they are tight, but the gap conducts heat eight times worse, and the rod is marked. Every rod goes through a helium leak detector. A leaking one (about one in three hundred) is cut open: the tube is scrapped and the pellets go back to the tray.

## The fuel assembly

The [assembly jig](item:promptcritical:assembly_jig) builds a skeleton of guide tubes and spacer grids and pulls rods of its own design into it:

- **VVER-440** — 126 rods in a hexagonal shroud, about 120 kg of uranium;
- **VVER-1000** — 312 rods in a hexagonal lattice, 18 guide tubes and a central tube, 15 grids, about 440 kg of uranium;
- **PWR 17×17** — 264 rods in a square lattice, 25 tubes, 11 grids, about 470 kg of uranium.

Rods of different enrichment can share an assembly; that is how assemblies are profiled, and the assembly's enrichment is the uranium-weighted mean. A finished assembly knows its type, uranium mass, enrichment and the gas under its claddings. It is the fuel for power reactors.
