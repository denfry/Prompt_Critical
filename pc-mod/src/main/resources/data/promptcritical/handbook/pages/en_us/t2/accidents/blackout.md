## Station blackout and the containment

The plant has AC on its safety buses while any ECCS pump or coolant pump has a turning shaft. When the grid drops and no diesel takes over, every one of them stops. That is a station blackout. The pumped ECCS and the normal makeup are gone and only the accumulators remain, which do nothing at full pressure.

If the steam generators (or the BWR's turbine drums) still carry the decay heat away, nothing happens: that is natural circulation, and a real plant keeps the generators fed with a steam-driven pump. If they do not, the closed loop climbs to the relief setpoint (17 MPa on the VVER, 7.9 MPa on the BWR) and boils its water off through the valves, dM/dt = −Q/h_fg. The VVER has about 5.6 t above the fuel and h_fg is 0.9 MJ/kg at 17 MPa, so on 1–2 MW of decay heat the core uncovers after about an hour. Get a shaft turning before then.

**Containment sizing.** The shell has to take everything that leaves the loop. A guillotine break on the VVER puts about 7 t of water and 9 GJ into it within a minute. The walls and the concrete soak up most of that, but a small building still pressurises: 500 m³ tears at 1 MPa, 4000 m³ holds. Plan on more than 500 m³ per tonne of primary water. Hydrogen goes into the same air, and at 8 % it burns. Each recombiner takes about 1.5 kg an hour at 4 %.

The boiling water reactor uses pressure suppression: the steam condenses in the pool and only hydrogen reaches the drywell. The containment is filled with nitrogen, so the hydrogen builds up but cannot burn.

A torn containment vents to the open air. From then on, iodine and caesium from failed fuel go out with the steam.
