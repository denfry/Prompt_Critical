## Core melt

If no water comes back, the uncovered core keeps heating on decay heat after the cladding melts. The bottom line of the VVER and BWR panels follows it.

**In the core.** At about 2100 K the zirconium that has not oxidised melts and runs down the fuel rods, freezing lower down and blocking the channels. At 2800 K the oxide fuel itself melts into a pool inside a crust. The panel shows this as lost geometry: the share of the core that has moved or melted. Water back over a core with less than 10 % lost geometry still quenches it, though it makes a lot of hydrogen as it does. A debris bed or a pool under water gives up only a few hundred kW per square metre, less than the decay heat of this small, dense core.

**The lower plenum.** Once about 40 % of the core is molten, the crust gives way and the melt drains into the lower plenum, as at TMI-2. If water is still standing there, the pour may set off a steam explosion: a chance in ten, worth 0.1–2 % of the melt's heat.

**The lower head.** The corium sends 0.4 of its decay heat down into the hemispherical head. If the reactor cavity is flooded, the 3×3 blocks under the vessel being water, boiling outside takes up to 1.2 MW/m² and the vessel holds. That is in-vessel retention; the heads of these small plants never come near that limit. A dry head heats up and fails by creep at 1300 K if the primary is above 10 MPa, at 1500 K if it is below 1 MPa. Failure at pressure blows the melt out as droplets, and a fifth of its heat goes straight into the containment gas. So open the **PORV** before the head goes.

**On the concrete.** Out of the vessel, the corium lands on the block under the reactor and eats down through the floor. One block of concrete takes 4.6 GJ, about 77 minutes of physical time at 1 MW. Water over the melt takes 0.4 MW/m² through its crust and can stop it. The water in the concrete comes back as hydrogen, about 13 kg per block, into the containment. Each block it sinks goes through the damage budget, so the server mode decides whether the world changes. The corium glows and carries the decay gamma of the core.
