Coal is not one substance but a mixture of combustible organic matter, mineral matter and water. Two pieces from different seams burn differently. So every piece of [run-of-mine coal](item:promptcritical:raw_coal) carries the **assay** of the place in the seam it came from. The assay is shown in the item tooltip. Pieces with different assays do not stack together.

## What the assay contains

- **Moisture, W.** Water must be driven off before coal will burn. Wet lignite spends a noticeable part of its heat doing that.
- **Ash, A.** The incombustible residue: clay, sand, shale. 12–30 % in run-of-mine coal. Ash gives no heat, clogs the grate and ends up as slag.
- **Volatile matter, V.** The share of mass driven off as gas when coal is heated without air. It is reckoned on a dry, ash-free basis, and coal is ranked by it.
- **Sulphur, S.** Burns to sulphur dioxide. Underground, pyrite turns mine water acidic.

## Ranks

The deeper a seam lay, the longer and hotter it was baked underground. Volatiles leave, carbon rises. This is **Hilt's law**: rank increases with depth.

- **Lignite** — more than 42 % volatiles. Wet and friable; boiler fuel.
- **Bituminous coal** — 8–42 % volatiles. This covers long-flame and gas coals for steam raising, fat and coking coals that form the base of a coke-oven blend, and the lean coals.
- **Anthracite** — under 8 % volatiles. Burns almost smokeless and is hard to light.

At Y = 0 volatiles are about 22 %, a coking coal. Coking coals lie deeper than steam coals. Ordinary Minecraft coal counts as a long-flame steam coal.

## What a seam looks like

A seam is a flat body 1–4 blocks thick and 48 to 160 blocks long with a gentle dip. Seams come in series, 6–20 blocks apart vertically. The seam floor is [fireclay](item:promptcritical:fireclay). Pyrite and sphalerite occur nearby.

## What the assay means for the plant

- **Boiler.** High ash and moisture mean less heat, more slag, more coal for the same steam.
- **Preparation.** [Jigging](promptcritical:i0/coal_prep) brings ash down to 6–10 %. Pyritic sulphur leaves with the heavy fraction; organic sulphur stays in the coal.
- **Mine.** A sulphurous seam, from 1.5 % sulphur, makes the mine water acidic. Dust from high-volatile coal explodes more easily and needs more stone dusting.
- **Coke.** The next epoch needs fat and coking coals with low ash and sulphur. Note the depth of your seams.
