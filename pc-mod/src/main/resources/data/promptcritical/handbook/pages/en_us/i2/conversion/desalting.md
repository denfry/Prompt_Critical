# Desalting

Crude from the wells carries brine: about 200 mg of salt per litre. In the column the magnesium and calcium chlorides hydrolyse to hydrochloric acid, which condenses with the water at the top and eats the tubes of the overhead cooler. Before distillation the salt must come down to 3–5 mg/l.

The [electrostatic desalter](item:promptcritical:electrostatic_desalter) washes the crude with fresh water in two stages. A mixing valve breaks the wash water into droplets so it meets the brine; then the emulsion settles in a field of 2 kV/cm between electrodes. The field polarises the droplets, they merge and the large drops sink fast (Stokes). Without voltage the drops stay small and almost no salt leaves: the electrodes need about 100 kW from the grid (a 6.3 kV cable to any face).

Salt after a stage is S·w_res/(w_in + η·r_w): the residual water w_res after settling over the water that has mixed with the crude. A harder valve mixes better (η rises from 0.5 to 0.9 between 0.05 and 0.15 MPa) but makes finer drops that settle worse. The optimum is near 0.1 MPa; the panel shows salt, water left in the crude and the valve drop.

Pipe crude and raw water to the sides or top; brine leaves from the bottom. Desalted crude does not go to a pipe: it flows straight into the nearest tube furnace within six blocks, and the furnace remembers how salty its crude is. Crude piped into the furnace past the desalter counts as raw.

The column panel shows the salt in its feed and the wear of the overhead tube bundle. On raw crude the bundle leaks after about 30 hours of play and a third of the gasoline is lost; desalted crude makes this practically never happen. Re-tube the cooler with four seamless pipes (use them on the column base).
