The I0 steam engine is a horizontal, double-acting, single-cylinder reciprocating engine. Steam from the boiler passes the stop valve and the governor throttle into the steam chest. The slide valve admits it first to one side of the piston, then to the other. Piston rod, crosshead and connecting rod turn the stroke into rotation of the crankshaft and flywheel.

## Power and steam

Steam is not admitted for the whole stroke. At **cut-off** the admission closes and the steam goes on working by expansion. The earlier the cut-off, the less steam per stroke and the better it is used.

At 120 rpm the I0 cylinder gives about 119 kW at the shaft and uses about 0.5 kg of steam a second. Part of the steam condenses on the cold cylinder walls, the curse of simple engines. A 5 MW boiler can feed about three such cylinders. One engine needs only a third of its steam, so the coal feed is matched to the load rather than held at rated.

## Coupling to Create

The engine is placed right against the boiler; it couples to the boiler whose block it touches. Rotation leaves through the shaft coupling into the Create network. A Create wrench on the coupling changes the gear: 16, 32 or 64 rpm at the shaft at rated engine speed. Here 1 Create stress unit is 1 kW. The engine delivers whatever the network draws, as long as there is steam.

## The stop valve

An empty-hand right-click on the engine opens and closes the stop valve. It is closed by default. Open it at no less than half of working pressure, about 0.5 MPa on the gauge. At low pressure an engine under load will not carry the crank over **dead centre**, the position where crank and connecting rod are in line and the piston gives no torque.

If the engine stops on dead centre, sneak and right-click with an empty hand: the driver bars it over 60° with a pinch bar. This works only on an engine that has nearly stopped.

## Watt's governor

Speed is held by a **flyball governor**: two weights on a spinning spindle fly outward as speed rises and close the throttle. It is the first automatic controller you will meet. It is proportional, with about 5 % **droop**: under load the engine runs a little slower than at no load. That is not a fault; every controller without integral action behaves this way.

A bare governor is poorly damped: after a load step the speed hunts up and down. The cure is a [governor dashpot](item:promptcritical:governor_dashpot), an oil damper. Fit it with a right-click on the engine. After that the speed settles within a couple of swings.

## What to watch

- A gauge on the engine reads steam chest pressure. Zero while the boiler holds pressure means the stop valve is shut.
- Speed falling under load more than usual means low boiler pressure, or a Create network asking more than one cylinder can give.
