## Containment

The containment is the last barrier between failed fuel and the outside air. Build it from containment wall blocks around the reactor, floor and roof included, within 24 blocks of the vessel. Get in and out through a personnel hatch; right-click opens and shuts it. The reactor checks the shell every five seconds. One open hatch or one missing block, and the fission products have a way out.

If the vessel bursts inside a closed shell, the blast still wrecks the room, but the iodine and caesium from failed fuel stay inside. The dose lands on whoever is in there. Without a shell, I-131 and Cs-137 from every failed assembly go up as a plume for a minute and drift downwind.

A hot core oxidises its zircaloy cladding in steam: Zr + 2H₂O → ZrO₂ + 2H₂, about 44 g of hydrogen per kilogram of zirconium, with heat on top (Baker–Just). Above roughly 8 % hydrogen in the containment air a global burn becomes possible. Passive recombiners take hydrogen out without power, about 1.5 kg an hour each at 4 %. Put them inside the shell.

Above 1204 °C (2200 °F) cladding embrittles and the core stops being coolable. That is the design limit the ECCS is built around: accumulators at about 4 MPa refill the vessel during blowdown, and low-pressure injection then keeps it flooded.
