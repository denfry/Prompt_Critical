The first-epoch boiler house is a fire-tube boiler, a chimney above it and a steam engine alongside. The engine sends rotation into the Create network. A chain grate stoker and a pressure gauge at the front are optional, but they make the job easier.

## What you need

- A [fire-tube boiler](item:promptcritical:fire_tube_boiler): 5 MW of heat, 1.2 MPa working pressure, about 8–10 t of water. It is placed whole, 3×3×6 blocks, empty and cold.
- A [chimney](item:promptcritical:chimney): sections stacked over the rear top edge of the boiler, above the flue. Chimney height is 3 m of flue plus 1 m per section. The reference is 20 m, about 17 sections.
- A [steam engine](item:promptcritical:steam_engine_cylinder): 3×3×5, one cylinder, about 119 kW at the shaft. It goes right against the boiler.
- Water: a bucket or Create pipes.
- Coal, and flint and steel, a torch or a fire charge.
- Optional: a [chain grate stoker](item:promptcritical:grate_stoker) and a [pressure gauge](item:promptcritical:instrument_pressure).

## The main lesson of lighting up

A boiler does not draw air by itself. Air is pulled into the furnace by the chimney's **draught**: the column of hot gas in the chimney is lighter than a column of outside air of the same height, and the pressure difference pulls air through the grate. The hotter the gas and the taller the chimney, the stronger the draught.

A cold chimney draws poorly. Load the grate to its rated duty straight away, three coal or 0.3 kg/s from the stoker, and there is not enough air. The coal burns to carbon monoxide instead of carbon dioxide. **Black smoke** pours from the chimney, efficiency drops to about 0.3, and the boiler takes more than half an hour to raise steam.

The right way is a **thin bed**. Start with one coal and add them one at a time while the smoke stays light. The chimney warms, the draught grows, and the firing rate is brought up to rated step by step. Done this way, the shaft turns for the first time about 20–25 minutes after an empty boiler.

Black smoke is an instrument. It tells you the furnace is short of air: stop firing.
