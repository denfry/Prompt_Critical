## Purpose

This procedure sets out how to heat up, run and hold a coke battery hot. Battery: 3–12 ovens 4–8 blocks long, silica brick flue walls, wall setpoint 1300 °C, coking time about 13.5 minutes.

## Before heat-up

- The battery is complete: right-clicking an oven opens the panel, not a "not formed" message.
- Every oven has a standpipe of the gas main, and the standpipes are joined.
- The exhausters touch the gas main; their Create shafts are connected but stopped.
- A pipe runs from the exhauster tank to the gas users: the battery for heating, the stoves, the boilers.
- The quench tower is built and piped for water.
- The larry car, pusher and quench car are built and tried out empty.
- Coal or coke for the warm-up fireboxes is at hand for the whole heat-up.

## Heat-up

- Load fuel into the warm-up fireboxes: the panel slot or the side faces of the ovens.
- Set the heat-up rate to 5 K/h or less. Up to 1000 °C nothing faster: the brickwork collects damage, shown in percent on the panel.
- Do not let the fireboxes run empty: cooling hot brickwork does more harm than slow heating.
- Above 1000 °C the rate may be raised.
- Once coke-oven or blast-furnace gas is available, pipe it into any block of the battery. Keep the fireboxes as a reserve.

## Start-up

- Charge ovens only when the walls are at 1000 °C or more: larry car over the charging holes, or a funnel into a hole.
- Charge the ovens one after another, not all at once: pushing will be evenly spread, and gas make will not jump.
- With the first charge, start the exhausters. Set their speed for +50…200 Pa in the gas main.

## Running

- Gas main pressure: +50…200 Pa. Bleeders burning means gas lost: raise suction or gas draw-off. Vacuum: cut exhauster speed at once.
- Push an oven soon after it is ready. Coke left too long loses strength.
- The quench car waits on the coke side before the push. Take hot coke straight to the quench tower.
- Send only quenched coke to the blast furnace. Check M25 and ash: coke below M25 80 breaks down in the blast furnace and makes the burden hang. The cause is the coal blend or overcoking.
- Hold the wall setpoint at 1250–1350 °C.

## Hot standby

- A battery is never let go out. The walls must never cool below 600 °C, whatever happens.
- For standby, push all the ovens and keep heating with a setpoint of at least 800 °C.
- With the exhausters stopped, gas goes to the bleeders; that is normal for an empty battery.

## Emergencies

- **Vacuum in the gas main.** Cut exhauster speed at once. 30 seconds below −50 Pa: air gets in and the main explodes.
- **Ram overloaded, cake stuck.** Withdraw the pusher. The oven finishes coking by itself; push once it is ready.
- **Burning coke on the ground.** There was no storage at the door. Bring the quench car up and put out the spilled coke.
- **No heating gas.** Switch heating to the warm-up fireboxes; do not let the walls cool.
- **Brickwork failed.** The battery is lost: take the ovens down, rebuild them, and start the heat-up from the beginning.
