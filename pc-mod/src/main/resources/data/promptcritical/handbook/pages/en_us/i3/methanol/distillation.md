## Distillation

Crude methanol from the separator is 80–85 % methanol; the rest is water from the CO₂ reaction plus traces of dimethyl ether and higher alcohols. The [methanol distillation columns](item:promptcritical:methanol_column) stand against the synthesis unit and take its crude together with steam for the reboilers: the converter shell steam and part of the heat from cooling the reformed gas.

The topping column strips dissolved gas and light ends. The refining column splits methanol from water over 30 theoretical trays. Near the top of the column methanol is 2.5 times more volatile than water, near the bottom 7.6 times, and the shortcut Fenske–Underwood–Gilliland method on the geometric mean of the two decides how clean the top product comes out. At the default reflux ratio of 1.5 water drops below 1000 ppm, which is grade AA (at least 99.85 % methanol, at most 0.10 % water). Push reflux below about 0.6 and the column can no longer separate: it runs on itself and holds the product back.

Steam limits throughput. Every mole of methanol taken overhead has to be boiled (1 + R) times, so a higher reflux means a cleaner product and fewer litres per hour. When the reboilers are short of steam, the Low steam lamp lights and crude backs up in the converter's separator.

Grade AA methanol leaves by pipe from any face. It is the same methanol the gas plant injects as hydrate inhibitor.
