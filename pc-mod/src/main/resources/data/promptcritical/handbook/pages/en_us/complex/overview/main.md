Prompt Critical is not about a single reactor. It is about a whole industrial complex, from a coal mine to a closed nuclear fuel cycle. Power, metallurgy, oil and gas and chemistry are not blocks that hand out energy here. They are plants with real physics behind them. A boiler builds pressure, a mine builds methane, a rope wears out, a casting comes out with a shrinkage cavity if you forget the riser.

Create provides the mechanics: shafts, gears, contraptions. The mod adds what drives those shafts and what they drive: the steam engine, the main mine fan, the hoist, pumps and machine tools.

## How progress works

Industries form a ladder. Each one is built on the products of the one before and supplies the next. The next step is unlocked by **materials**, not by quests or research points. No steel, no pipes; no pipes, no oil; no electricity, no graphite for a reactor.

The first step is **coal and steam**. Find a coal seam, mine the coal, build a boiler and a steam engine. The Create shaft that steam sets turning is where the whole plant begins.

## Using the handbook

- Chapters open by themselves when you do things: place a block, obtain an item, earn an advancement. A locked chapter shows a hint.
- The handbook never blocks anything. You can build without it; it just costs less with it.
- The handbook explains the physics and teaches you to read instruments, but it **never shows** the current values of your plant. Pressure is read from the gauge, gas from the lamp and the methanometer.
- Many chapters end with a "What to do next" line.

## Operating procedures

When you build a boiler, a mine hoist or a main mine fan, you receive an [operating procedure](item:promptcritical:operating_procedure). It is the same handbook chapter in the form of a document. Put it on a [document lectern](item:promptcritical:document_lectern) next to the plant, and anyone nearby can read the procedure. Real plants work the same way: the procedure sits at the operator's station.

## The cost of mistakes

Mistakes are expensive here. A boiler that has lost its water explodes, an unventilated mine explodes, a cage on a worn rope falls. Every such accident has a cause, and the instruments nearly always showed it in advance. The handbook teaches you to notice it in time.
