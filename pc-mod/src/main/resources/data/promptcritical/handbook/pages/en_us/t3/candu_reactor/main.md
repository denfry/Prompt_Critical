## The heavy water reactor

CANDU burns natural uranium — 0.711 % U-235, no enrichment. Heavy water makes that possible: deuterium hardly absorbs neutrons, so there are enough of them for a chain reaction even in natural uranium.

The plant is 3×6×3. Below is the calandria, a tank of cold heavy water (70 °C) crossed by 97 horizontal channels. Each channel holds 12 bundles of 37 elements and about 19 kg of uranium per bundle. The coolant is D₂O too, at 10 MPa and 266 → 310 °C. Above is the fuelling machine bridge.

Within 12 blocks of the desk you need coolant pumps and steam generators against secondary boilers.

Neutrons live about a millisecond here, thirty times longer than in a VVER: slowing down in D₂O is slow and diffusion is long.

The coolant void coefficient is **positive**: voiding every channel adds +10…+15 mk. Heavy water in the channel moderates little and absorbs a bit, so losing it only adds reactivity. Hence the fast shutoff rods, 2 s per stroke.

**Tritium** builds up in the moderator: deuterium catches a neutron and becomes T. It is a pure beta emitter with no external dose; the danger is a water or steam leak, where it is breathed in and swallowed. The desk counts it in GBq.
