The graphite pile got by on uranium metal from a lab distillation of yellowcake. Reactors and an enrichment cascade need industrial chemistry: pull four kilograms of uranium out of a tonne of ore, purify it and turn it into a gas, uranium hexafluoride (UF₆). That is the front end. Every stage keeps count on its panel of the uranium that came in, went on and was lost.

## Acid and hydrogen fluoride

The [sulphuric acid plant](item:promptcritical:acid_plant) roasts pyrite, oxidises SO₂ to SO₃ over vanadium catalyst and absorbs it in acid. Sulphur content comes from the ore assay. Equilibrium at the last bed outlet sets how much SO₂ converts: 97–98 % at 430 °C, less when hotter. Below 400 °C the catalyst goes out. Unconverted SO₂ goes up the stack.

The [hydrogen fluoride kiln](item:promptcritical:hf_kiln) breaks down fluorspar with sulphuric acid at 200–250 °C. Take anhydrous HF from the bottom. If it has nowhere to go, the condenser vents it, and a hydrogen fluoride cloud drifts downwind.

## Leaching

The [leach train](item:promptcritical:leach_tank) is a cascade of agitated tanks with thickeners. Ore goes in batches (50 kg each, 0.4 % uranium), with acid and water. Barren solution from sorption can replace the water. Pitchblende is uranium(IV) dioxide, and only uranium(VI) dissolves. Ferric iron oxidises it, and pyrolusite re-oxidises the iron. Without pyrolusite only a third of the uranium opens up; with it, 90–93 %. Heating to 60–70 °C speeds things up several times over. Pregnant solution (1 g/l uranium) leaves from the top and tailings slurry from the bottom. The slurry carries some uranium with it: what never dissolved and what was not washed out.

## Sorption

The [ion exchange unit](item:promptcritical:uranium_ion_exchange) runs two columns of anion resin. The resin is made from pyrolysis gasoline and chlorine (styrene copolymer, chloromethylation and amination folded into one recipe). The uranyl sulphate complex sticks to the resin, and everything else passes into the barren solution. A loaded column is eluted with acidified brine, which gives 10 g/l uranium in the eluate. While one column elutes, the solution goes to the other. If both are busy, the solution bypasses the resin and the uranium is lost.

## Yellowcake

The [precipitator](item:promptcritical:precipitation_tank) adds liquid ammonia to the eluate, and ammonium diuranate settles out: yellowcake. It takes about 0.55 kg of ammonia per kilogram of uranium, part of it to neutralise the acid. Short of ammonia, the pH never reaches 7 and the uranium stays in solution.

## Conversion

Dry conversion runs in three furnaces. In the [rotary calciner](item:promptcritical:rotary_calciner) at 400–500 °C the cake becomes UO₃. In the [reduction furnace](item:promptcritical:reduction_furnace) at 600–700 °C hydrogen strips oxygen to give UO₂. In the [hydrofluorination reactor](item:promptcritical:hydrofluorination_reactor) at 400–600 °C UO₂ with hydrogen fluoride gives UF₄, green salt. Each furnace has its own temperature window: below it the reaction is slow, above it the powder sinters.

## Fluorine and UF₆

Fluorine comes from electrolysis of molten KF·2HF at 80–100 °C in the [fluorine cell series](item:promptcritical:fluorine_cell). The same electrolysis rectifier as the chlorine cells supplies the current. A cell runs at 9–10 V, because the fluorine overvoltage on a carbon anode is large. A kilogram of fluorine costs 13–15 kWh. The melt uses up HF, and the controls top it up. If the HF runs out, a fluorographite film grows on the anode: voltage jumps by 20 V and fluorine all but stops. That is the anode effect.

The [flame reactor](item:promptcritical:fluorination_reactor) burns UF₄ in fluorine. UF₆ freezes out in cold traps and drains into a cylinder: a 48Y for 12.5 t or a small 5A for 25 kg. Load an empty cylinder into the reactor and take the full one from the bottom. The "Ship" button sends a partly filled one on. Without a cylinder the trap overflows and UF₆ leaves with the vent gas. In moist air it hydrolyses at once: a kilogram of UF₆ gives 0.23 kg of hydrogen fluoride, and that cloud is dangerous downwind.

From ore to cylinder, 85–90 % of the uranium gets through. The leach tailings take the most; conversion loses less than one per cent. From here a 48Y of natural UF₆ goes on to enrichment.
