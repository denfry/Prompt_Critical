## Purpose

This procedure sets out how to start and run the main mine fan and the rules for ventilating the workings. The fan draws air out of the mine to the surface. Fresh air enters through the other openings and must pass through every working face.

## Before start-up

- The 3×3×3 fan stands in the mouth of a heading: one side open to the outside, the other into the mine.
- A Create shaft is brought in from behind to the coupling on the fan axis. Rated shaft speed is 64 rpm.
- Ventilation doors are placed so that air cannot short-cut from the intake to the fan but has to sweep the faces.
- All doors are closed.
- Canary cages with birds are in place at the faces.
- A gas monitoring cabinet is installed in the mine: an empty-hand click on it gives a summary.

## Start-up

- Turn the fan shaft.
- Check the air intake on the cabinet summary.
- Walk the headings with a lamp or methanometer. Make sure the air current passes every face.

## Running

- The fan runs **continuously** while the mine has any open seam, even with nobody underground.
- Methane in the return from a face: no more than 1 %; in the mine return: no more than 0.75 %; local accumulations: no more than 2 %.
- Air velocity: at least 0.25 m/s; on a longwall face, no more than 4 m/s.
- Open doors only to pass through and close them at once.
- Ventilate new headings and dead ends at once, before work starts.
- Test for methane at the roof: gas gathers high.

## Shutdown

- Stop the fan only when the mine is empty.
- Before people go back: start the fan, wait, test methane with the methanometer at every face.

## Emergencies

- **Fan stopped.** Everyone to fresh air and to the surface. In a dead-end heading 1 % methane builds in tens of seconds, in the mine as a whole in minutes. Do not strike an open flame.
- **Gas monitoring cabinet in alarm.** Methane somewhere has reached 1 %. Stop work in the mine, locate it from the summary and the methanometer, increase the ventilation.
- **Canary down.** The heading has carbon monoxide or too little oxygen. Put on a self-rescuer and head for fresh air. Carry the bird out if it is still alive.
- **Lamp: flame inside the gauze.** Methane is above 5 %. Leave at once; do not shake the lamp or carry it through a fast air current.
- **After an explosion.** Doors are blown open, supports damaged, afterdamp in the headings. Work only on a self-rescuer. Restore the doors, clear the gas, check the supports.
