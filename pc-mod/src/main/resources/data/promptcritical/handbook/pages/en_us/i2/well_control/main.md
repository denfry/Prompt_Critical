As long as bottomhole pressure stays above pore pressure, the formation holds back. Drill into an overpressured bed on light mud and gas, oil or water flows into the well. That is a kick, and a kick nobody controls becomes a blowout.

## Warning signs

The best sign is pit gain. Whatever the formation gives up pushes the same volume of mud up into the pits. The alarm goes off at 1.6 m³ (10 barrels). The second sign is flow with the pumps off. Gas expands as it rises, so the gain grows faster and faster.

## Shutting in

At the first sign press BOP. The annular preventer seals the annulus, and the pumps and weight on bit come off. Pressures settle in a minute or two. The drill pipe holds clean mud, so SIDPP is exactly the missing hydrostatic: pore pressure minus ρgH. Casing pressure reads higher, because the light influx sits in the annulus.

Don't wait too long. Gas migrates up a shut-in well at about 300 m an hour and carries formation pressure with it, so both gauges climb. The casing limit is MAASP: above it the rock at the casing shoe breaks down. Above 34.5 MPa the preventer itself gives way.

## The driller's method

1. Well shut in, pumps stopped: press Kill. The console records SIDPP and SICP and fills in a kill sheet: kill mud ρ₀ + SIDPP/(gH), initial circulating pressure ICP = slow circulating pressure + SIDPP, final FCP = slow circulating pressure · ρ_kill/ρ₀.
2. First circulation: the pump comes up to 30 strokes a minute on its own and the automatic choke holds ICP on the standpipe. The influx goes out through the separator, and bottomhole pressure stays at pore pressure plus annular friction. Near the end the gas expands at the surface and casing pressure peaks.
3. Weight up: pour [barite](item:promptcritical:barite_powder) into the [mud mixing hopper](item:promptcritical:mud_mixing_hopper), 50 kg a sack. Create millstones grind it from [bedded barite](item:promptcritical:barite_ore), which lies in the cap rock above the traps. Press Kill again.
4. Second circulation: while kill mud travels to the bit, drill pipe pressure steps down from ICP to FCP, then holds FCP. The old mud goes to a reserve tank.
5. Once kill mud reaches the surface the pump stops and trapped pressure is bled off through the choke. If SIDPP and SICP both read zero, open the preventer. The well should not flow.

## Blowout

If gas reaches the surface with the preventer open while the formation is still flowing, you have a blowout. Gas on the rig ignites, and the cloud explosion goes through the damage budget. From then on the well just flows.

## The BOP stack and the accumulator

A rig needs a [BOP accumulator unit](item:promptcritical:bop_accumulator) within its radius. It is a rack of eight 41.6 l bottles with nitrogen precharged to 7 MPa and hydraulic fluid pumped up to 21 MPa. Boyle's law gives what a bottle can deliver before the pressure falls to the 8.3 MPa a preventer needs to close: V(7/8.3 − 7/21), about 21 l. Closing the annular takes 68 l, a set of rams 22 l. API Std 53 asks for enough fluid to close everything and open the hydraulic valve with the pumps off and still keep 1.4 MPa over precharge; the API 53 lamp shows whether the stack passes. A Create shaft on the right-hand port recharges the bottles in 15 minutes.

Each element strokes for 30 seconds before it seals.

- The [annular preventer](item:promptcritical:bop_annular) seals on any pipe. After 20 closures its packer is worn and holds only 80 % of 34.5 MPa. A dried kelp block used on the accumulator replaces the packer of an open annular.
- A [ram preventer](item:promptcritical:bop_ram) carries pipe, blind or shear rams; change them with an empty hand while it is open. Pipe rams seal only on 5″ pipe. Blind rams do not close on pipe at all. Shear rams cut the pipe body and seal the hole, but stall on a tool joint: joints come every 9.144 m. A cut string is lost, and the rig can no longer circulate.

Test the stack with the Test lever: every open element is closed and opened again. An element untested for 14 days fails to seal one closure in twenty.
