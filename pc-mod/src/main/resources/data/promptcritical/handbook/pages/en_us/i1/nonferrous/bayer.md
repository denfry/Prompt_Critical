Aluminium is made in two steps. First the Bayer process pulls pure alumina (Al₂O₃) out of bauxite with hot caustic soda. Then electrolysis splits the alumina in molten cryolite. A tonne of aluminium needs 1.93 t of alumina, and a tonne of alumina needs 4–5 t of bauxite.

## Digestion

The [Bayer digester](item:promptcritical:bayer_digester) is an autoclave with a mud thickener. Load bauxite by hand or hopper; its Al₂O₃ comes from the assay (50 % without one). Pipe caustic soda and fuel gas into the sides. The gas raises the autoclave steam. Aluminate liquor leaves from the top, red mud from the bottom.

The liquor circulates at 200 g/l NaOH. After the autoclave its caustic ratio αk (Na₂O to Al₂O₃, by moles) is 1.7, which is about 150 g/l of alumina. After precipitation it is back at 3.5 (73 g/l). Each litre therefore carries 77 g of alumina from the bauxite to the seed.

Temperature decides what dissolves. Gibbsite goes into solution from 140 °C, boehmite only from 240 °C. The bauxite seam here is a mix, half and half. At 145 °C you get the gibbsite and need about 4.4 t of bauxite per tonne of alumina. At 245 °C you get both and need 2.1 t, but the steam bill doubles. Below 100 °C nothing dissolves.

Reactive silica in the bauxite turns into sodium aluminosilicate and takes caustic and alumina into the mud with it: 50–100 kg of NaOH per tonne of alumina. That is the make-up caustic you buy from the chlorine plant. Red mud is a pH 10–13 slurry. It belongs in a lined pond. The Ajka dam failure in 2010 is what happens when that pond breaks.

## Precipitation

The [precipitator](item:promptcritical:alumina_precipitator) holds 20 m³ of liquor, which is 30 physical hours of residence. Seeded with hydrate at 50–70 °C, the liquor drops 95 % of its extra alumina as aluminium hydroxide. Outside that window the yield falls: colder crystals grow too slowly, hotter liquor holds more in solution. The hydrate comes out in 100 kg bags at the bottom. Spent liquor goes back to the digester from the top as caustic, counted by NaOH mass.

## Calcination

The [rotary calciner](item:promptcritical:alumina_calciner) drives the water out of the hydrate at 1000–1100 °C. Its fuel is pipeline gas, about 3.1 GJ per tonne of alumina. Below 1000 °C the product keeps water and the kiln slows down. Hotter only costs gas.
