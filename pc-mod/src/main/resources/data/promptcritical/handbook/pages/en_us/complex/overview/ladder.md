## The ladder of industries

An epoch is a step for the whole complex, not for one machine. Old industries do not disappear: a plant in the atomic epoch still mines coal and melts steel, because everything else is made from them.

- **I0. Coal and steam.** A coal mine with methane, roof support and drainage, coal preparation, a coal-fired boiler, a steam engine driving a Create shaft, a foundry and the first machine tools. Gives coal, steam and the first mechanisms.
- **I1. Steel and power.** Coke ovens, blast furnace, converter, rolling mill and seamless pipe; synchronous generator, lines and transformers; water treatment and air separation. Gives steel, pipe, electricity, coke and pitch for graphite, oxygen and nitrogen.
- **I2. Oil and gas.** Exploration, drilling, production, pipelines, an oil refinery and a gas plant, gas turbines. Gives fuels, sulphur, helium, oils and petroleum coke.
- **I3. Chemistry.** Pyrolysis, polymers, chlorine and caustic soda, hydrogen and syngas, ammonia, ion-exchange resins. Gives reagents, resins, PTFE and electronics.
- **I4. Nuclear.** Uranium enrichment, fuel assemblies, the reactor, a closed fuel cycle, isotopes. Gives base-load power.

Each epoch opens with the products of the previous one. A graphite reactor needs electricity and pitch, electricity needs steel, steel needs coke, coke needs coal. The road to the atom starts at the coal face.

## The automation ladder

The control system grows alongside, from a handwheel to plant-wide management.

- **A0.** Manual control: valves, levers, local instruments such as a pressure gauge.
- **A1.** Relay logic: relays, contacts, timers.
- **A2.** Analogue computers: summers, integrators, controllers.
- **A3.** Programmable controllers and safety instrumented systems.
- **A4.** Distributed control and SCADA on computers.
- **A5.** Plant level: planning and optimisation.

In I0 you work at A0, with your hands and eyes. The flyball governor of the steam engine is the first automatic controller you will meet.
