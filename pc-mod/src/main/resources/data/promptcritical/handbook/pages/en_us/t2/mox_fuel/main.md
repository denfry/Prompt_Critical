## Plutonium back into the core

The plutonium that comes out of PUREX does not have to wait for a fast reactor. Blended into depleted or reprocessed uranium at the pellet press, it makes MOX fuel, and every thermal reactor on the site will burn it in its own geometry:

- **T1, AM-1 channel**: an AM-1 channel assembly pressed from MOX pellets;
- **T2a and T2b, VVER/PWR vessel and BWR**: a VVER-440, VVER-1000 or PWR 17×17 assembly;
- **T3, CANDU**: 37-element bundles. The bundles in the refuelling machine's magazine average together, so uranium and MOX bundles can be mixed.

The limit is the same everywhere: **at most 12 % plutonium** in the heavy metal. Above that the void coefficient of a light-water lattice turns positive, and no licence would allow it. A richer assembly does not fit the slot. Plants run at 5–8 % Pu; the press mixes 7 %.

The reactor keeps the plutonium as Pu-239, Pu-240, Pu-241 and Pu-242 in every assembly, not as some "equivalent uranium". Pu-239 and Pu-241 fission. Pu-240 eats neutrons and slowly turns into Pu-241. Pu-241's own captures make Pu-242. Pu-238 is carried with the Pu-242, since neither fissions much on thermal neutrons.

The plutonium vector therefore matters. Weapons-grade or lightly burned plutonium is worth more than plutonium from a high-burnup discharge with a quarter Pu-240 in it. The tooltip shows the lot's Pu share and its Pu-239 content.

Unloaded MOX carries the remaining plutonium, isotope by isotope, back to PUREX.
