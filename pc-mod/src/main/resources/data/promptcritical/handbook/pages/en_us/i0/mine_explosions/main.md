An explosion in a coal mine is nearly always a double one. Methane ignites, the blast wave lifts settled coal dust, and from there a dust explosion runs through the workings. It can travel hundreds of metres from where the gas was.

## Methane

A methane–air mixture explodes between 5 and 15 %, most violently at about 9.5 %. Below 5 % there is not enough gas to burn, above 15 % not enough oxygen. The danger zone is not only inside an accumulation: a rich pocket is diluted by air at its edges and passes through the explosive range.

## Sources of ignition

- **Open flame.** A torch, lantern, candle, campfire, fire, lava or magma in a heading with methane can set off an explosion. In a gassy mine, light it with [industrial lights](item:promptcritical:industrial_light), not torches.
- **The lamp.** A [flame safety lamp](item:promptcritical:flame_safety_lamp) is safe while its gauze is intact. At 5 % methane and above, in an air current faster than 4 m/s, the flame is blown through the gauze.
- **Sparks** from electrical equipment without gas protection, and shotfiring.

## Coal dust

Dust settles on the floor and sides; cutting produces it, the shearer most of all. Lying there it is harmless, but a blast wave lifts it into the air. If there is enough dust and too little inert material in it, the flame front keeps going.

The more volatiles in the coal, the more dangerous its dust. Anthracite dust hardly explodes at all. For bituminous coal the settled dust needs 60–80 % inert material before it stops carrying an explosion: the more volatiles, the more inert.

## Stone dusting

Stone dust is ground limestone. [Stone dust](item:promptcritical:stone_dust) is made from calcite or diorite. With a [stone duster](item:promptcritical:stone_duster) and dust in your inventory, right-click in a heading: it spreads 100 kg and shows the inert fraction at that spot. Dust regularly: fresh coal dust settles on top of the old.

## Barriers

An [explosion barrier](item:promptcritical:explosion_barrier) is a set of shelves loaded with stone dust under the roof. The blast tips them over, and the cloud of inert dust quenches the flame. A barrier works when there are at least 400 kg of dust per square metre of the heading's cross-section. One barrier block holds about 500 kg. Place barriers across headings between the working faces and the rest of the mine.

## After an explosion

- The main danger is **afterdamp**: 1–8 % carbon monoxide and less than 10 % oxygen. It kills more men than the blast itself. Put on a self-rescuer and head for fresh air.
- The blast breaks supports and throws ventilation doors open, short-circuiting the air.
- Restore the doors, check the supports, clear the gas from the headings. The canary and the methanometer will tell you when it is safe to go back.

The mine is not lost after an explosion. But find the cause before you start production again.
