An instrument does not say "good" or "bad". It shows a number, and the operator decides. To decide quickly, read the scale by bands.

## Scale bands

- **Normal.** The operating range. On modern control panels (the ISA-101 standard) it is deliberately plain and grey: the eye should not catch on things that are fine.
- **Warning.** The value has left the normal range, but there is time to act. Find the cause.
- **Alarm.** A protection is about to trip or already has. Act by the procedure.

On a good panel, colour appears only for abnormal values. When everything on a panel is bright, operators stop noticing what matters.

## Read more than the number

- **Which way the needle is going.** 0.9 MPa and rising is a different situation from 0.9 MPa and falling.
- **How fast.** A slow rise is a boiler warming up. A fast one means something was closed or steam stopped being drawn.
- **Look square on.** Read the needle from the side and you are off by a division (parallax).
- **Check zero.** On a cold boiler with no pressure, the needle should rest on zero.

## Why the needle lags

A pressure gauge is a Bourdon tube: a curved, flattened tube that straightens under pressure and pulls the needle through a small linkage. The tube and linkage have inertia, so the needle trails the pressure by about half a second. A sharp step shows up smoothed, and a short spike may not show at all. The gauge shows what was there a moment ago.

## The boiler gauge

The [pressure gauge](item:promptcritical:instrument_pressure) is an A0 instrument: local, no wiring. Hang it on a face of the boiler or the steam engine and it reads the gauge pressure of whatever it hangs on.

- On the boiler it reads the steam space.
- On the engine it reads the steam chest, downstream of the stop valve. If the boiler holds pressure while the engine gauge reads zero, the stop valve is shut.

The scale is 0–1.6 MPa over 270°: zero at the lower left, the needle moving clockwise. The red mark is the boiler's working pressure. The working point sits in the last third of the scale on purpose, so that both normal pressure and the margin up to the valve setting read clearly.

## Other "instruments" of the first epoch

- The boiler's gauge glass: a column of water with a red mark at the lowest permitted level.
- Smoke colour at the chimney: light means enough air, black means the furnace is starved.
- The safety valve's whistle: pressure has reached the set point.
- Underground: the canary, the flame of the safety lamp and the methanometer. See [The mine: ventilation and methane](promptcritical:i0/mine_ventilation).
