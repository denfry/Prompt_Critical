## The pressurizer

The loop is full of water everywhere except the pressurizer, which keeps a steam bubble above its water. Heat the coolant and it swells: the surplus enters the pressurizer, squeezes the bubble and the pressure rises. Cool it and the water drains back, the bubble expands and the pressure falls.

What holds the pressure:

- **heaters** warm the pressurizer water. The proportional group runs all the time against heat losses; the backup groups come on below 15.55 MPa;
- **spray** from the pump discharge knocks the bubble down above 15.8 MPa, full flow at 16.1 MPa. No pumps, no spray;
- the **relief valve** opens at 17.0 MPa, the **safety valve** at 17.6 MPa. Steam goes to the relief tank and the loop loses water.

Charging holds the level: up to 6 kg/s against a 1 kg/s letdown.

A load rejection raises pressure and level; a load increase drops them. Power follows the steam by itself: colder coolant means more reactivity.
