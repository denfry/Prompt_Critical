# Amine treating

Associated gas carries hydrogen sulphide, here 2 % by volume, and some carbon dioxide. Sales gas may hold no more than 20 mg of H₂S per cubic metre. The [amine unit](item:promptcritical:amine_unit) washes it out with a 45 % solution of methyldiethanolamine (MDEA), charged into the unit when it is built.

The amine unit stands within six blocks of the gas plant and treats all the gas the plant takes in. In the absorber H₂S reacts with the amine at once; CO₂ reacts slowly, so most of it slips through and the acid gas stays rich in H₂S. In the stripper a reboiler boils the H₂S back out and the amine returns lean.

Circulation must carry the acid gas: Q = n/(Δα·C), with 3.93 kmol of amine per m³ and the loading rising from lean to at most 0.5 mol per mol. Too little amine and it saturates, and H₂S slips into the sales gas. The reboiler steam sets how lean the amine comes back: 110 kg per m³ gives a loading of 0.01, 140 kg gives about 0.006, and the cleaner the lean amine, the less H₂S leaves with the gas. The reboiler here is fired with sales gas piped to any face, with the same heat as that steam.

The hydrogen sulphide collects in the unit and goes to a Claus unit that touches it; the sulphur goes on to the acid plant and uranium leaching. Without a Claus unit the acid gas has nowhere to go and the amine stops absorbing. The panel shows H₂S in the treated gas, the rich loading, circulation, steam and the purity of the acid gas.
