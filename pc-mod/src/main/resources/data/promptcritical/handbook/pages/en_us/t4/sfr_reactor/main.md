## The sodium-cooled fast reactor

No moderator: the neutrons stay fast, and a fast neutron hitting U-238 makes plutonium more often than the core burns it. The core is a single MOX assembly with 15–35 % plutonium, surrounded by a uranium blanket. With 18 % Pu the breeding ratio is about 1.2: the core itself loses plutonium, the blanket gains more than that.

The plant is 3×6×3: the sodium pool with the core below, the rotating plug with the rod drives above. Within 12 blocks of the desk you need:

- two **pumps** on Create shafts: the first drives the primary sodium, the second the intermediate loop;
- a **steam generator** with a water-tube boiler drum: the intermediate sodium gives its heat to water there. No steam generator, no heat sink: the core outlet climbs to the 580 °C trip.

Fast kinetics is touchy. The delayed neutron fraction of plutonium is about 0.0035 against 0.0065 for uranium, and the neutron generation time is 0.4 µs. One dollar is only 350 pcm; withdraw the rods slowly and watch the period.

What holds the power is fuel Doppler (weaker than in a VVER), axial fuel expansion and the radial expansion of the core support plate as the inlet heats up. In this small core, sodium voiding is slightly negative overall, but voiding only the centre adds reactivity. In big cores like BN-600 the centre wins.


## Station blackout and decay heat removal

When the station loses its own power, the sodium pumps coast down, the steam generators stop taking heat and the reactor trips. The fission stops, the decay heat does not: right after the trip it is several per cent of the power, and it falls slowly. In a sodium reactor that is not dangerous at once: the pool holds about eighty tonnes of sodium at atmospheric pressure, nothing boils off, and sodium circulates through the core by itself, on the difference in density between hot and cold. The heat goes away through air heat exchangers on the intermediate loops: three trains, half a megawatt each with the intermediate loop at its working temperature. Their dampers open on the scram by themselves, and air draws through them without fans. With them the core outlet stays well below the cladding limit. Without them the pool heats up by tens of degrees an hour. In about seven hours the sodium at the core outlet passes 700 °C, and the steel cladding of the fuel pins starts to fail: the fuel and steel form a low-melting eutectic, and the steel creeps. Near 883 °C the sodium boils in the centre of the core, and there the void effect is positive. The panel shows the air coolers' power, the boiling and the share of failed cladding. Fission products from failed pins stay in the sodium as long as it stays in the pool. A sodium fire carries them out with the smoke.
