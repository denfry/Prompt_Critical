## The sodium-cooled fast reactor

No moderator: the neutrons stay fast, and a fast neutron hitting U-238 makes plutonium more often than the core burns it. The core is a single MOX assembly with 15–35 % plutonium, surrounded by a uranium blanket. With 18 % Pu the breeding ratio is about 1.2: the core itself loses plutonium, the blanket gains more than that.

The plant is 3×6×3: the sodium pool with the core below, the rotating plug with the rod drives above. Within 12 blocks of the desk you need:

- two **pumps** on Create shafts: the first drives the primary sodium, the second the intermediate loop;
- a **steam generator** with a water-tube boiler drum: the intermediate sodium gives its heat to water there. No steam generator, no heat sink: the core outlet climbs to the 580 °C trip.

Fast kinetics is touchy. The delayed neutron fraction of plutonium is about 0.0035 against 0.0065 for uranium, and the neutron generation time is 0.4 µs. One dollar is only 350 pcm; withdraw the rods slowly and watch the period.

What holds the power is fuel Doppler (weaker than in a VVER), axial fuel expansion and the radial expansion of the core support plate as the inlet heats up. In this small core, sodium voiding is slightly negative overall, but voiding only the centre adds reactivity. In big cores like BN-600 the centre wins.
