Instruments and messages in the mod use SI units. Here is what you will meet in the first epoch and how to translate it into something familiar.

## Pressure

- **Pa, kPa, MPa.** 1 MPa = 1000 kPa = 10 bar ≈ 145 psi. Atmospheric pressure is about 0.1 MPa.
- **Absolute and gauge.** Absolute pressure is measured from vacuum, gauge pressure from the atmosphere. A pressure gauge reads gauge pressure: open to the air, its needle sits at zero. Absolute = gauge + 0.1 MPa.
- The I0 boiler works at 1.2 MPa; the safety valve lifts at 1.1 times working pressure. The 0.1 MPa difference between absolute and gauge matters at these numbers: go by the red mark on the gauge rather than converting in your head.
- **Pa in a mine.** A mine fan develops a few hundred pascals. That is a thousandth of a megapascal, yet it moves tens of cubic metres of air a second.

## Temperature

°C and K differ by an offset: K = °C + 273.15. A temperature difference is the same in °C and K. The tin core of a fusible plug melts at 232 °C; saturated water at the boiler's working pressure is around 190 °C.

## Power and energy

- **kW and MW.** The I0 boiler delivers about 5 MW of heat; the steam engine about 119 kW at the shaft. The gap is the efficiency of the whole chain: thermal power and shaft power are not the same thing.
- **Create stress.** In this mod 1 Create stress unit (SU) = 1 kW. A 119 kW engine gives the network about 119 SU; fans, pumps and machine tools draw stress according to the power they actually use.

## Flow rates

- **kg/s** for fuel: the chain-grate stoker feeds 0.3 kg/s at rated speed.
- **m³/s** for mine air; **m³/h** for drainage water.
- **t/h** for steam and coal in bulk.

One item of bulk material, such as coal, slag or stone dust, weighs 100 kg.

## Concentrations

- **%** by volume for methane and oxygen. Clean air is 20.9 % oxygen. Methane explodes between 5 and 15 %.
- **ppm**, parts per million, for toxic gases such as carbon monoxide (CO). 1 % = 10,000 ppm.

## Time

The boiler house, steam engine, hoist, machine tools and shearer run in **real time**: a second of play is a second of physics. Slow processes, such as gas in the workings, roof collapse, mould cooling and the cupola melt, are sped up: one game day (20 minutes) equals one day of physics, a factor of 72. That is why methane builds up behind a stopped fan in minutes rather than hours.
