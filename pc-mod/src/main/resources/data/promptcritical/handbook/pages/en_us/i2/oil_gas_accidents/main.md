Oil and gas plants hurt people in a handful of well-known ways. The mod runs each one through the same chain as a reactor accident: release, cloud or pool, ignition, consequence. Explosions go through the damage budget, gas through the plume model, spills into the emission ledger. Server mode decides how much of it reaches the world: creative_safe shows smoke and sound only.

## Leaks and clouds

Gas leaking in the open drifts downwind as a Gaussian plume. Propane and hydrogen sulphide are heavier than air: their cloud hugs the ground and fills hollows and basements. Only the part between the lower and upper flammable limits can burn (methane 4.4–17 %, propane 1.7–10.9 %). Gas detectors and the portable detector read that cloud in % LEL. Sales gas that has not passed an odorizer has no smell: only a detector sees it.

## Ignition

Whether a leak lights depends on its size (Cox, Lees and Ang): for gas 1 % under 1 kg/s, 7 % up to 50 kg/s, 30 % above. Two ignitions in five are immediate: a jet fire at the hole. The rest are delayed: the cloud grows first, then finds a source. Fire, lava and lit furnaces light it at once; turning Create machinery and grid equipment with a 10 % chance a second; powered redstone 2 %. Explosion-proof equipment (gas detectors, the LPG bullet) does not spark.

## Vapour cloud explosion and flash fire

A delayed ignition among columns and pipe racks speeds the flame up into a blast. Only the cloud inside congested space counts: places where at least 30 % of a 5×5×5 cube is solid. The charge is 3.5 MJ per cubic metre; congestion sets the strength class (4, 6 or 8; 10 for hydrogen and ethylene) and the share that becomes blast. The open part burns as a flash fire: whoever stands in the cloud burns. Either way the flame then runs back to the leak and keeps burning as a jet fire.

## Jet and pool fires

A 100 MW jet fire is about 22 m long and puts 100–250 kW/m² on anything in its flame. A burning tank or spill burns 0.02–0.055 kg/(m²·s) of fuel; a 10 m pool of gasoline releases 189 MW. Radiant heat hurts: 4 kW/m² is pain (half a heart a second), 12.5 kW/m² sets wood alight, 37.5 kW/m² ruins steelwork. Get out of sight of the flames. Water does not put out burning oil: use foam.

## BLEVE

An LPG bullet in a fire is the classic disaster. The fire heats the liquid until the relief valve lifts; the vented propane often burns as a torch over the valve. Nothing cools the wall above the liquid: at about 535 °C the steel no longer holds the relief pressure and the bullet bursts. The boiling liquid makes a fireball (5.8·M^⅓ m across, 0.45·M^⅓ s long: 20 t of propane gives 158 m and 12 s) and a blast wave. In a pool fire this takes about ten minutes, under a jet fire about five. A deluge nozzle or fire monitor running at the bullet keeps the wall below 300 °C: fit one and watch the wall temperature on the panel.

## Hydrogen sulphide

Sour gas kills. Above 100 ppm you can no longer smell it, just when it gets dangerous. A burning leak turns it into SO₂, the lesser evil.

## Oil spills

On land a spill becomes up to 64 blocks of crude oil that flow slowly and burn as a pool fire. On water it spreads into a slick (Fay): 10 m³ covers 2·10⁴ m² in an hour, 0.5 mm thick. The light ends evaporate (Fingas) and count as VOC in the emission ledger; the rest takes up water and turns into mousse. The slick oils sand and gravel on the shore.

Clean-up: booms on the water stop the slick, and a closed boom makes it thicker. The skimmer needs a Create shaft and collects Q·h/(h + 2 mm), so it hardly takes a thin film: boom first. A sorbent pad takes 15 kg of oil from a slick block or cleans an oiled shore block, and turns into oiled sorbent for burning. An oiled shore also weathers by itself, halving in about 30 days.
