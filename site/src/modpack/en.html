Modpack · design preview · Minecraft 1.21.1 · NeoForge

From a coal pit to Mars

A planned modpack built on Prompt Critical. It is meant to be longer and harder than GregTech: New Horizons, with the length coming from distinct stages instead of grinding. Each era below asks you to understand a new process, design a plant for it and keep that plant running. Eras I0 to I4 are already in the mod. The pack itself, its acceptance rules and era licences, and the space eras are still in design.

Stages, not grind

When you get stuck, the way forward is a process you haven't designed yet. There are no counters to fill.

Commissioning gates

Build anything. A unit of a new kind starts only after its tests pass on the real equipment and its instructions are written.

Team or solo

A team splits shifts and rounds. A solo player automates the same work on CC computers. The rules are the same.

One physics

The same thermal, hydraulic and grid models run everywhere. On Mars only the boundary conditions change.

I0

Coal and steam

in the mod

A shaft, a boiler and a steam engine turning a Create shaft. Methane in the mine is real, and so is the fusible plug in the boiler.

Branches

  • Mine
  • Boiler and steam
  • Foundry and machine shop

New problem

Ventilation and drainage underground; draft, water level and pressure in a fire-tube boiler.

Acceptance

Open from the start. The first checklists already exist: hydrotest, relief valve setting, instrument check, trial run.

I1

Steel and power

in the mod

Coke ovens, a blast furnace, a converter and a rolling mill. Then a synchronous generator, lines, transformers and the air separation unit.

Branches

  • Coke and iron
  • Steel and rolling
  • Power grid
  • Water and boilers
  • Control
  • Graphite pile

New problem

Synchronising a generator to the grid, setting protection relays, keeping the furnace hot enough and the steel clean enough.

Acceptance

Insulation and alignment tests on rotating machines, protection proof tests, the first era licence for your team.

T0graphite pile on natural uranium: criticality and delayed neutrons
I2

Oil and gas

in the mod

Seismic survey, a drilling rig, a field, pipelines, a refinery and a gas plant. Gas turbines join the grid.

Branches

  • Drilling and production
  • Refining
  • Gas
  • Plant control
  • Pipelines and market

New problem

Holding a kick with the blowout preventer, mud weight against formation pressure, columns that separate what you designed them for.

Acceptance

Inert leak tests and purging before gas goes in, loop checks for the plant control system.

T1channel reactor after the AM-1 at Obninsk, the first nuclear power plant: xenon, the iodine pit, burnup by channel
I3

Chemistry

in the mod

Pyrolysis and polymers, chlor-alkali, hydrogen and syngas, ammonia, ion-exchange resins. The electronics of the transistor age come out of this era.

Branches

  • Organics
  • Inorganics
  • Power and electronics

New problem

Reactions that run away if you let them, corrosive media that eat the wrong steel, safety systems that must trip before the operator does.

Acceptance

Flushing, proof tests of the safety instrumented systems, written start-up and shutdown instructions checked against the real tags.

T2–T3VVER/PWR and BWR, then heavy-water reactors with refuelling on power
I4

Nuclear fuel

in the mod

Ore to enrichment, pellets to fuel assemblies, spent fuel back through reprocessing. Isotopes and RTGs on the side.

Branches

  • Ore and enrichment
  • Fuel
  • Radiation
  • Closing the cycle

New problem

Dose, shielding and decontamination; xenon after a power drop; a fuel cycle that has to close on itself.

Acceptance

Rod drop time, containment leak rate L ≤ 0.75 La, diesel generator load acceptance. A reactor that fails any of them does not start.

T4sodium fast reactor on MOX from your own spent fuel

Then the pipe leaves the atmosphere

Rockets are built the way NASA and SpaceX build them: parts with quality passports, engine test firing, assembly on the pad, flight software you write on CC computers. A stock program flies too, just worse. One server clock, transfers on a ×1000 scale: about 4 minutes to the Moon, about 6 hours to Mars.

I5

Orbit

in design

Sounding rockets, satellites, a crewed flight, a station like the ISS and a tracking network on the ground.

You build

  • Engine test stands
  • Launch pad and mission control
  • Observatories and tracking stations
  • Station modules

New problem

No convection in vacuum, so heat leaves only through radiators. Life support is a plant of its own: oxygen, CO₂, water, leaks, fire.

Body

Real microgravity on the station: handrails, a jetpack, a tether outside.

I6

Moon and fusion

in design

Terrain from NASA elevation data. Helium-3 in the regolith is the reason to go, and fusion is what it is for.

Specialty

Helium-3, oxygen and titanium from ilmenite, ice in polar craters.

New problem

A night that lasts two weeks. A cheap launch off a small body makes the Moon a fuel stop for everything further out.

Body

A sixth of Earth's gravity, a suit, oxygen and dose to watch.

I7

Mars and asteroids

in design

Mars from MOLA data at full width: Gale crater, Valles Marineris and the polar caps are where they really are.

Specialty

Water with five to seven times Earth's deuterium, methane and oxygen from the air by the Sabatier reaction; platinum metals and nickel-iron on asteroids.

New problem

Air at 0.6 % of Earth's pressure, nothing to burn, a weaker sun, dust storms. Your boiler from I0 has to be designed again.

Logistics

A launch window about every 18 hours of real time. Miss it and the cargo waits.

I8

Outer system

in design

Hydrocarbon lakes on Titan and helium-3 from the gas giants. Beyond that, interstellar flight, which honest physics makes a matter of decades.

Agencies

Every team is a space agency. Firsts are written into the server's history once.

Trade

One open docking standard. Propellant, helium-3 and heavy water change hands between teams.

Back to Earth

Only what is rare, light and valuable. Colonies mostly supply space itself.
